# APUSH — Short Answer Question (SAQ) Guide
**Section I, Part B | 40 minutes | 20% of score**
**Hub:** [[../APUSH Master Study Hub]] | **Also:** [[DBQ Guide]] · [[LEQ Guide]] · [[MCQ Stimulus Guide]]

---

## Format

- **3 questions answered** in 40 min (~13 min each)
- Q1: Required — secondary source stimulus, **1754–1980**
- Q2: Required — primary source stimulus, **1754–1980**
- Q3 OR Q4 (your choice): Q3 covers **1491–1877**; Q4 covers **1865–2001**
- Each question has **Parts A, B, C** — usually 2–4 sentences per part
- **No thesis required** — SAQs are direct, evidence-based responses
- Bullet points acceptable; complete sentences preferred

---

## Scoring

Each SAQ is worth **3 points** (1 point per part):
- Part A: usually "Briefly describe" or "Briefly explain" — 1 specific, accurate historical claim
- Part B: usually "Explain one cause/effect/similarity/difference" — 1 claim + specific evidence
- Part C: usually "Explain one way X continued/changed" or "Evaluate the extent" — 1 claim + different evidence than Part B

---

## Template — Response Structure

**For each part:**
> [Claim sentence stating your answer directly.] [Evidence sentence with a specific example — name, date, event, act, or person.] [Optional: 1 sentence of explanation connecting evidence to the claim.]

Keep it tight. Graders spend ~30 seconds per part. Front-load the claim.

---

## Stimulus Types

### Secondary Source (Q1)
- A historian's argument or interpretation
- Part A often asks: "Briefly describe ONE perspective presented in the excerpt"
- Part B often asks: "Briefly explain ONE cause of the development described in the excerpt"
- Part C often asks: "Briefly explain ONE way [a later development] challenged or extended the argument"

> [!tip] Secondary Source Approach
> Identify the historian's main claim in the first sentence of your Q1-A response. Then use Parts B and C to bring in your own outside evidence — the excerpt is a jumping-off point, not the whole answer.

### Primary Source (Q2)
- A historical document, speech, letter, image, or political cartoon
- Focus on who wrote it, when, to what audience, and for what purpose
- Parts B and C will ask for outside historical evidence beyond the document

---

## Common SAQ Prompts and Approaches

| Prompt Type | Strategy |
|---|---|
| "Briefly describe ONE cause of..." | Name a specific cause; explain the mechanism |
| "Briefly explain ONE effect of..." | Name a specific effect; show it followed from the cause |
| "Briefly explain ONE similarity/difference between..." | State the comparison directly; use two specific examples |
| "Briefly explain ONE way [X] changed between [date] and [date]" | Anchor to a specific turning point event |
| "Briefly explain ONE way [X] continued..." | Identify a persistent pattern; use a specific example from the later period |

---

## Worked Example Structure

**Prompt:** "Briefly explain ONE cause of the American Revolution."

> **Part A response:** One cause of the American Revolution was the British Parliament's imposition of new taxes on colonists without their representation. The Stamp Act of 1765 required colonists to pay a direct tax on printed materials, provoking widespread colonial protest under the slogan "no taxation without representation." This challenged the colonists' understanding of their rights as British subjects.

---

## Traps to Avoid

> [!warning] Vague evidence kills points
> "Colonists were angry about taxes" earns 0. "The Stamp Act of 1765 imposed a direct tax on printed goods, sparking the Sons of Liberty protests" earns 1. Be specific.

> [!warning] Don't write an essay
> SAQs are not mini-LEQs. No intro paragraph, no thesis, no conclusion. Answer the part directly and move on.

> [!warning] Time management
> 40 min / 3 questions = ~13 min each. Parts A and B usually take ~3–4 min each; Part C ~5 min. Don't spend 20 minutes on Q1.
