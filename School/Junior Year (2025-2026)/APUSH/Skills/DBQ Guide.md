# APUSH — Document-Based Question (DBQ) Guide
**Section II, Part A | 60 min (15 reading + 45 writing) | 25% of score**
**Hub:** [[../APUSH Master Study Hub]] | **Also:** [[LEQ Guide]] · [[SAQ Guide]] · [[MCQ Stimulus Guide]]

---

## Overview

- 1 question with **7 documents** (primary sources: texts, images, maps, charts)
- Focus period: **1754–1980**
- Must use documents to support a historical argument
- Maximum score: **7 points**

---

## Scoring Rubric (7 points total)

| Category | Points | What's Required |
|---|---|---|
| **Thesis/Claim** | 1 | Defensible claim beyond restating the prompt; establishes a line of reasoning |
| **Contextualization** | 1 | Describes a broader historical context *before the prompt period* and connects it to the argument — must be a full explanation, not a mention |
| **Evidence: Document Content** | 1–3 | Uses content from at least 3 docs (1 pt) OR at least 6 docs with explanation of how each supports argument (3 pts) |
| **Evidence: Outside Evidence** | 1 | Provides specific evidence *not* in the documents that supports the argument |
| **Analysis: Sourcing** | 1 | Explains how a doc's historical situation, audience, purpose, or point of view is relevant to your argument — for at least 3 docs |
| **Analysis: Complexity** | 1 | Demonstrates nuanced understanding (qualify, corroborate across periods, explain both sides, etc.) |

> [!danger] Contextualization Trap
> A single sentence like "This took place during a time of great change" earns 0. You need a full paragraph explaining the *relevant* prior context and explicitly connecting it to the prompt argument. Aim for 3–5 sentences.

---

## 15-Minute Reading Strategy

1. Read the prompt — identify the **exact argument** you're being asked to make
2. Skim all 7 documents — group them by what position they support (pro/con/neutral)
3. Note each doc's: **historical situation**, **audience**, **purpose**, **point of view** (HAPP)
4. Identify which 3 docs you'll do full sourcing analysis on
5. Think of 1–2 outside evidence examples not in the docs
6. Draft your thesis mentally before writing

---

## Essay Structure

### Paragraph 1 — Contextualization + Thesis
- Open with **2–4 sentences of contextualization** (earlier period, broader trend, preceding event)
- State how that context connects to your argument
- End with your **thesis**: a defensible claim with a clear line of reasoning

> [!tip] Thesis Formula
> "Although [counterargument/nuance], [your argument] because [reason 1] and [reason 2]."
> Example: "Although economic factors contributed to colonial discontent, British political decisions after 1763 most fundamentally caused the American Revolution because they directly undermined colonial self-governance and violated established constitutional precedents."

### Body Paragraphs (2–3 paragraphs recommended)
- Each paragraph = 1 category of your argument
- For each document you use:
  1. Introduce and summarize the document's relevant content
  2. Explain how it supports your argument
  3. Do HAPP sourcing analysis for at least 3 docs

**HAPP Sourcing Formula:**
> "This document was written by [author], who [historical situation]. Because [audience/purpose/POV], the document [explains why it supports or limits your argument in this way]."

- Weave in **outside evidence** in at least one paragraph

### Final Paragraph (optional but recommended for complexity)
- Qualify your argument: acknowledge an exception or counterargument
- OR connect to a broader/later period
- This is where you earn the complexity point

## HAPP Quick Reference

| Element | What to Explain |
|---|---|
| **Historical Situation** | What was happening at the time the document was created? |
| **Audience** | Who was the intended reader/viewer? How does that shape the content? |
| **Purpose** | Why was this document created? What was the author trying to achieve? |
| **Point of View** | How does the author's background, identity, or position shape their perspective? |

> [!warning] Don't just label — explain
> "This was written by a plantation owner (point of view)" earns 0. "Because this was written by a plantation owner whose wealth depended on enslaved labor, his argument against emancipation reflects economic self-interest rather than a neutral assessment of slavery's morality" earns 1.

## Outside Evidence Examples by Era

For **1754–1800 DBQs:** Proclamation of 1763, Committees of Correspondence, Thomas Paine's *Common Sense*, Shays' Rebellion, Federalist No. 10
For **1800–1865 DBQs:** Missouri Compromise, Nullification Crisis, Uncle Tom's Cabin, Dred Scott decision, Lincoln's House Divided speech
For **1865–1920 DBQs:** Reconstruction Amendments, Plessy v. Ferguson, Homestead Strike, Muckraking journalism, 19th Amendment
For **1920–1945 DBQs:** Red Scare, Harlem Renaissance, Smoot-Hawley Tariff, Civilian Conservation Corps, Executive Order 9066
For **1945–1980 DBQs:** Truman Doctrine, NSC-68, Brown v. Board, Civil Rights Act 1964, Gulf of Tonkin Resolution, Nixon Doctrine

## Traps to Avoid

> [!warning] Don't summarize — argue
> Every document use should connect to your thesis. "Document 3 shows that..." is a summary. "Document 3 demonstrates that [your argument], because..." is analysis.

> [!warning] Contextualization ≠ background
> Context must come from *before* the time period in the prompt AND explicitly connect to your argument. For a 1900 prompt: don't just describe 1890s events — explain *how* they shaped the conditions the prompt is asking about.

> [!warning] Use 6 documents for maximum points
> You need at least 6 documents with explanation (not just mention) for the 3-point evidence score. Aim for 6; treat the 7th as backup.
