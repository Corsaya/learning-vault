# APUSH — Long Essay Question (LEQ) Guide
**Section II, Part B | 40 minutes | 15% of score**
**Hub:** [[../APUSH Master Study Hub]] | **Also:** [[DBQ Guide]] · [[SAQ Guide]] · [[MCQ Stimulus Guide]]

---

## Overview

- Choose **1 of 3 prompts** (one per era):
  - Era 1: **1491–1800** (Units 1–3)
  - Era 2: **1800–1898** (Units 4–6)
  - Era 3: **1890–2001** (Units 7–9)
- All prompts test the same historical reasoning skill (causation, comparison, or CCOT) — the era changes, not the skill type
- Maximum score: **6 points**
- No documents provided — this is all from memory

---

## Scoring Rubric (6 points total)

| Category | Points | What's Required |
|---|---|---|
| **Thesis/Claim** | 1 | Defensible claim that establishes a line of reasoning; not just restatement of prompt |
| **Contextualization** | 1 | Explains broader historical context *connected* to the argument (full explanation, not a mention) |
| **Evidence: Specific** | 1–2 | 1 pt for relevant specific examples; 2 pts for using specific evidence to support your argument |
| **Analysis: Reasoning** | 1–2 | 1 pt for using the skill correctly; 2 pts for using it to explain the thesis throughout the essay |

> [!warning] Complexity (bonus thinking, not a scored point in LEQ)
> Unlike the DBQ, LEQ has no complexity point. All 6 points come from thesis, context, evidence, and reasoning. Focus your 40 minutes on those four categories.

## 40-Minute Time Plan

| Time | Task |
|---|---|
| 0–5 min | Read all 3 prompts; choose your era; brainstorm evidence (quick list) |
| 5–8 min | Draft thesis and outline (topic sentence per paragraph) |
| 8–35 min | Write essay: intro with context + thesis → 2–3 body paragraphs → brief conclusion |
| 35–40 min | Review thesis and topic sentences for coherence |

## Essay Structure

### Introduction (3–5 sentences)
1. **Contextualization** (2–3 sentences): Describe a relevant development from *before* the prompt's time period, explained fully and connected to your argument
2. **Thesis** (1–2 sentences): Defensible claim with a clear line of reasoning

> [!tip] Thesis Formula
> **Causation:** "[X] occurred primarily because of [cause 1] and [cause 2], which [explain connection]."
> **Comparison:** "While [Group A] and [Group B] both [shared trait], they differed significantly in [difference 1] and [difference 2], reflecting [broader historical point]."
> **CCOT:** "Between [date] and [date], [topic] changed significantly due to [factor], while [element] remained constant, demonstrating [historical significance]."

### Body Paragraphs (2–3 paragraphs)
Each paragraph = 1 supporting argument
- **Topic sentence** that connects directly to the thesis
- **Specific evidence**: named events, people, acts, documents, dates — the more specific, the better
- **Analysis**: explain how the evidence supports your argument using the assigned reasoning skill

**For causation:** explain the mechanism — how did Cause X lead to Effect Y?
**For comparison:** explicitly state the similarity/difference and explain what it reveals
**For CCOT:** anchor changes to specific turning points; anchor continuities to persistent patterns with examples from multiple points in the period

### Conclusion (1–3 sentences, optional but good practice)
- Restate thesis in different words
- Note broader significance or connection to another period

## Common Reasoning Skill Prompts

### Causation LEQ
"Evaluate the extent to which [X] caused [Y]."
"Explain the causes of [X]."
→ Organize by cause type (political, economic, social); argue which was most significant

### Comparison LEQ
"Compare [Group A] and [Group B] in the period [dates]."
"Evaluate the extent to which [X] and [Y] were similar."
→ Choose 2–3 specific criteria for comparison; address both similarity AND difference

### CCOT LEQ
"Evaluate the extent to which [X] changed between [date] and [date]."
→ Identify specific turning points for change; identify elements that persisted; argue the overall balance

## Traps to Avoid

> [!warning] Vague thesis = 0 points
> "The American Revolution was caused by many factors" is not a thesis. "The American Revolution was caused primarily by British political decisions that threatened colonial self-governance, particularly Parliament's post-1763 taxation policies and the Intolerable Acts" is a thesis.

> [!warning] Contextualization ≠ restating the prompt
> "In the period 1754–1800, the American colonies fought for independence" is not context. "The Seven Years' War (1754–1763) fundamentally transformed British-colonial relations by doubling Britain's debt and leading Parliament to impose new taxes on colonists who had grown accustomed to decades of salutary neglect" IS context.

> [!warning] Evidence without analysis = 1 pt, not 2
> Listing events earns the basic evidence point. Explaining *how* each event supports your thesis argument earns the second evidence point. Always explain the connection.
