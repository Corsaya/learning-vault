## Study Hub Quick Reference — Unit 9

> [!check] MCQ Pattern — Reagan Revolution
> Reagan represents a sharp ideological break from the liberal consensus (New Deal → Great Society). Key Reagan arguments: government is the problem, not the solution; cut taxes, deregulate, rebuild military. MCQ may ask about the conservative shift or use Reagan's speeches as stimuli. Know both his stated ideology and the gap between rhetoric and reality (deficits ballooned; government didn't shrink much).

> [!warning] Trap — End of Cold War Isn't All Reagan
> Conservative narrative: Reagan's military buildup bankrupted the USSR and won the Cold War. Historical consensus: the Cold War ended due to a combination of Reagan's pressure, Gorbachev's reforms (glasnost, perestroika), and internal Soviet structural collapse. Don't accept either extreme.

> [!warning] Trap — Unit 9 Is Low-Weight But Has LEQ Coverage
> Unit 9 is only 4–6% of MCQ but **LEQ Era 3 (1890–2001)** covers through 2001, meaning you may need Reagan, Gulf War, or 9/11 context in a long essay. Know the big picture even if you don't memorize every detail.

## The Reagan Revolution (1981–1989)

**Conservative Coalition:**
- **New Right**: religious conservatives (Moral Majority — Jerry Falwell), economic conservatives, anti-communist hawks, Southern white voters (realignment after Civil Rights Act)
- **Election of 1980**: Reagan defeated Carter in landslide; blamed Carter for stagflation, Iran hostages, Soviet expansion; "Are you better off than you were 4 years ago?"

**Economic Policy ("Reaganomics" / Supply-Side Economics):**
- **Tax cuts**: Economic Recovery Tax Act (1981) — top marginal rate from 70% to 28%; benefited wealthy most
- **Deregulation**: reduced federal oversight of businesses, banks, environment
- **Defense spending**: massive military buildup → drove up budget deficit (tripled national debt)
- **Social spending cuts**: reduced welfare programs, food stamps, public housing
- **Results**: inflation dropped sharply; economy recovered from stagflation; BUT income inequality increased dramatically; budget deficits exploded; national debt tripled

**Foreign Policy:**
- **Reagan Doctrine**: support anti-communist insurgencies worldwide; funded mujahideen (Afghanistan), Contras (Nicaragua), UNITA (Angola)
- **Iran-Contra Affair**: U.S. illegally sold arms to Iran (hostage deal) and diverted profits to Nicaraguan Contras (Congress had banned Contra funding); major scandal; Reagan "did not recall"
- **Military buildup**: 600-ship Navy; Strategic Defense Initiative ("Star Wars") — missile defense system; put pressure on Soviet military spending
- **Grenada invasion (1983)**: overthrew pro-Cuban government; first U.S. military victory since WWII (small-scale but symbolically significant)
- **Intermediate-Range Nuclear Forces Treaty (1987)**: Reagan and Gorbachev; eliminated entire class of nuclear weapons — first time weapons were actually eliminated (not just limited)

## End of the Cold War (1989–1991)

- **Mikhail Gorbachev**: Soviet leader 1985; launched **glasnost** (openness) and **perestroika** (economic restructuring) to save USSR; inadvertently unleashed forces that destroyed it
- **Revolutions of 1989**: Soviet satellite states democratized peacefully; **Berlin Wall fell November 9, 1989**
- **Soviet Union dissolved December 25, 1991**: 15 independent republics; Russia the successor state
- **George H.W. Bush** managed the transition; German reunification; Start treaties

**Gulf War (1991):**
- Iraq under Saddam Hussein invaded Kuwait (August 1990)
- Bush assembled international coalition (34 nations including Arab states); UN authorization
- **Operation Desert Storm (January–February 1991)**: 100-hour ground war; Kuwait liberated; Bush stopped short of Baghdad (no UN mandate to go further; feared chaos)
- Demonstrated U.S. military dominance in post-Cold War world; "New World Order"

## 1990s: Globalization and the Clinton Era

**Clinton (1993–2001):**
- **"Third Way" / New Democrat**: shifted Democrats toward center; "end of big government"
- **NAFTA (1993)**: North American Free Trade Agreement; eliminated tariffs with Canada and Mexico; supported by Clinton; opposed by labor unions (job outsourcing) and Ross Perot
- **Budget surplus**: by 1998, first balanced budget since 1969; economy boomed (tech bubble)
- **Welfare Reform (1996)**: Personal Responsibility and Work Opportunity Act; "end welfare as we know it"; replaced AFDC with TANF; added work requirements; block grants to states
- **Globalization**: WTO (1995); trade deficits; deindustrialization of Rust Belt; wage stagnation for working class despite overall growth
- **Kosovo intervention (1999)**: NATO bombing campaign stopped Serbian ethnic cleansing; no U.S. casualties; Clinton doctrine of humanitarian intervention

**Domestic:**
- Crime Bill (1994): 100,000 new police; mandatory minimums; later criticized for mass incarceration
- Technology revolution: internet, dot-com boom; tech stocks collapsed 2000–01
- **Impeachment (1998)**: Clinton lied under oath about affair with Monica Lewinsky; impeached by House; acquitted by Senate

## 9/11 and the War on Terror (2001–Present)

- **September 11, 2001**: Al-Qaeda hijacked 4 planes; destroyed World Trade Center, hit Pentagon; ~3,000 killed; largest terrorist attack in U.S. history
- **PATRIOT Act (2001)**: expanded government surveillance; reduced privacy protections
- **Department of Homeland Security (2002)**: largest federal reorganization since 1947
- **Afghanistan War (October 2001)**: removed Taliban government that harbored Al-Qaeda; longest U.S. war (ended 2021)
- **Iraq War (2003)**: Bush administration claimed Saddam Hussein had WMDs (never found); removed Hussein; occupation descended into insurgency; sectarian violence; depleted U.S. resources and credibility
- **Bush Doctrine**: preemptive war; spread democracy by force; unilateral action if needed

**George W. Bush Domestic:**
- Tax cuts (2001, 2003): benefited wealthy; increased deficits
- **No Child Left Behind (2002)**: federal education standards and testing requirements
- **Hurricane Katrina (2005)**: delayed federal response exposed poverty, racial inequality, and government incompetence; eroded public trust in Bush

## Contemporary Trends (2008–Present) — Overview

**Obama (2009–2017):**
- First Black president; historic election
- **Affordable Care Act (2010)**: expanded health insurance coverage; individual mandate; most significant domestic legislation since Great Society; polarizing
- **2008 Financial Crisis**: housing bubble collapse; bank bailouts (TARP); Great Recession; unemployment peaked ~10%; stimulus package (American Recovery and Reinvestment Act)
- Ended Iraq combat operations; escalated Afghanistan (surge); killed Osama bin Laden (2011)
- Increasing partisan polarization; Tea Party movement (conservative backlash)
- *Obergefell v. Hodges* (2015): legalized same-sex marriage nationally

**Key Long-Term Trends:**
- **Income inequality** rose throughout this period (Gini coefficient); middle class squeezed
- **Deindustrialization**: manufacturing decline; Rust Belt; service economy growth
- **Immigration**: demographic shift; Hispanic population growth; immigration as political flashpoint
- **Partisan polarization**: geographic and cultural sorting of political parties; decline of political moderates

## AP Exam Tips — Unit 9

- **Low-weight unit (4–6%)** — most MCQ sets won't center here, but expect 2–4 questions
- **LEQ Era 3 (1890–2001)**: know Reagan and Cold War's end as possible content; "Evaluate the extent to which Reagan's policies transformed the U.S. economy" is a real LEQ type
- **SAQ Q4 (choice)** covers 1865–2001 — Unit 9 is in scope; a prompt about post-Cold War foreign policy or the Reagan Revolution is possible
- **Continuity and change**: trace the arc from New Deal liberal consensus → conservative backlash (Reagan) → third-way center (Clinton); great CCOT evidence
- **Complexity**: Reagan cut taxes AND increased deficits; proclaimed smaller government AND grew military spending; Cold War "won" was also Gorbachev's reforms. Always add the nuance.
