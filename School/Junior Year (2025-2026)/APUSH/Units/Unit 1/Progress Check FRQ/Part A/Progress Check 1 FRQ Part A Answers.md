# Unit 1 — Progress Check FRQ Part A Answers
**Period: 1491–1607**

---

## FRQ Type: SAQ (Short Answer Question)
The SAQ asks you to respond in complete sentences to two or three directed parts (a, b, c), each worth one point. No thesis is required. Each part typically asks you to *describe* or *explain* one specific historical claim, development, cause, or effect. The skill is precision — a focused claim plus one piece of specific evidence per part earns full credit. Bullet-point lists alone are not acceptable.

---

## Excerpts Used

**Excerpt 1 — Isabel de Guevara, wife of a Spanish conquistador, petition to the Spanish monarch, 1556:**
> "Several women came to this province of the Río de la Plata [in South America] along with its first governor Don Pedro de Mendoza, and it was my fortune to be one of them. On reaching the port of Buenos Aires, our expedition contained 1,500 men, but food was scarce, and the hunger was such that within three months 1,000 of them died. . . . The men became so weak that all of the tasks fell on the poor women, washing the clothes as well as nursing the men, preparing them the little food there was, keeping them clean, standing guard, patrolling the fires, loading the crossbows when the Indians came sometimes to do battle. I was left out, being old and single and in a condition of poverty because of my encomienda . . . because most of [the land] was distributed among the Spaniards here, . . . and I was left out without being given the service of a single Indian. . . . So I beg you to order that my encomienda be granted to me in perpetuity."

**Excerpt 2 — Chief of the Acuera Indians of present-day Florida, Spanish account of the chief's response to Hernando de Soto's demand that he submit to the Spanish king, 1539:**
> "I am king in my land and it is unnecessary for me to become the subject of a person who has no more subjects than I. I regard men as vile and contemptible when they subject themselves to the yoke of someone else when they could instead live as free men. . . . I do not wish to know what your sovereign demands. . . . All of you should go away as quickly as you can if you do not want to perish at my hands."

---

## Question Prompt
Using the excerpts, answer (a) and (b):

(a) Briefly describe ONE claim made in the excerpt.
(b) Briefly describe ONE historical development illustrated by the excerpt.

---

### Part A
**Task:** Briefly describe ONE claim made in the excerpt.

**Full answer:**
Isabel de Guevara claims that Spanish women made essential military and logistical contributions to the Buenos Aires expedition that were equal to or greater than those of the men, yet were excluded from the encomienda grants that rewarded male colonizers. Alternatively, the Chief of the Acuera asserts that submitting to Spanish authority would be an act of dishonor incompatible with his status as a free king, and that Native leaders had both the right and the will to resist Spanish demands militarily.

**To earn full credit:**
- Make ONE specific, defensible claim drawn from either excerpt — don't paraphrase vaguely ("the excerpt says women worked hard")
- Name the source and specify what the claim is
- Complete sentences required; a bulleted list alone earns no credit

---

### Part B
**Task:** Briefly describe ONE historical development illustrated by the excerpt.

**Full answer:**
The excerpts together illustrate the pattern of Native American resistance to Spanish colonization in the early contact period. The Chief of the Acuera's defiant refusal to submit to de Soto's authority in 1539 reflects the broader historical reality that many Native leaders actively resisted Spanish demands for submission and tribute, using both military force and rhetorical assertion of sovereignty. Alternatively, the de Guevara excerpt illustrates the development of the encomienda system as the primary mechanism for distributing labor and rewards in Spanish colonial America, and the gendered exclusion from that system that left women — even those who contributed to conquest — without formal recognition or economic reward.

**To earn full credit:**
- Identify a specific *development* (a pattern, system, or trend), not just restate what the excerpt says
- The development must be historically grounded — connect it to a larger trend (colonization, encomienda, resistance)
- One clear sentence is enough if it's specific
