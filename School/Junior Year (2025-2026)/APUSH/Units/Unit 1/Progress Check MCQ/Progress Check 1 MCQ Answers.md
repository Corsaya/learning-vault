## Question 1
> Questions 1 through 3 refer to the following excerpt:
> "On the western side of the ocean, movements of people and ideas . . . preceded the Atlantic connection. Great empires—in the Valley of Mexico, on the Mississippi River . . . —had collapsed or declined in the centuries before 1492. As Columbus embarked on his first transatlantic voyage, the Mexico, or Aztecs, were consolidating their position [in Mexico]; their city was a center of both trade and military might. Tenochtitlan [the Aztec capital] . . . held 200,000 people, a population greater than in the largest city in contemporary Europe. ". . . The Mississippian culture spread east and west from its center, the city of Cahokia, on the Mississippi River near the site of modern St. Louis. It was a successor to earlier cultures, evidence of which can be seen in the great ceremonial mounds they built. Cahokia declined and was ultimately abandoned completely in the later thirteenth century. . . . Throughout the Southeast, smaller mound-building centers continued."
> — Karen Ordahl Kuppermann, historian, *The Atlantic in World History*, 2012
>
> Which of the following contributed most significantly to the population trend in pre-Columbian Mexico described in the excerpt?
>
> A. Migration in pursuit of fertile lands
> B. Trade and settlement resulting from maize cultivation
> C. Low birth rates and high death tolls as a result of European diseases
> D. Internal conflict between groups causing political instability

**Notes section:** Unit 1 — Native American Societies Before European Contact

**Key concept:** This question tests your understanding of what drove the growth of large, complex societies in pre-Columbian Mesoamerica — specifically the role of agriculture (maize) and trade networks in sustaining dense urban populations like Tenochtitlan.

**Correct answer: B — Trade and settlement resulting from maize cultivation**
The excerpt describes Tenochtitlan as "a center of both trade and military might" with a population exceeding any contemporary European city. This scale of urban settlement was made possible by the agricultural revolution of maize cultivation, which produced surpluses that supported dense populations and enabled the trade networks that connected Mesoamerican civilizations.

**Why the others are wrong:**
- **A:** Migration toward fertile lands describes earlier, nomadic periods — not the settled, urbanized civilizations the excerpt explicitly describes.
- **C:** European diseases are entirely anachronistic here; the excerpt is describing pre-Columbian trends, before Columbus arrived.
- **D:** The excerpt notes that Cahokia *declined*, but it attributes this to internal collapse, not population growth. Conflict explains decline, not the large populations the question is asking about.

## Question 2
> (Same excerpt — Kuppermann)
>
> Which of the following best characterizes the Mississippian societies described in the excerpt?
>
> A. They had mixed agricultural and hunter-gatherer economies that favored the development of permanent villages.
> B. They were nomadic peoples who utilized river systems to move throughout the region.
> C. They lived in isolated, impermanent communities and left very little trace of their presence once a settlement had been abandoned.
> D. They used advanced agricultural practices like irrigation to support economic growth.

**Notes section:** Unit 1 — Native American Societies Before European Contact

**Key concept:** This is a close-reading question testing whether you can accurately characterize the Mississippian culture from the excerpt rather than applying outside knowledge or assumptions.

**Correct answer: A — They had mixed agricultural and hunter-gatherer economies that favored the development of permanent villages.**
The excerpt describes Cahokia as a major urban center with ceremonial mound-building, indicating permanent, complex settlement. The phrase "successor to earlier cultures" and the mound evidence both point to sedentary societies with agricultural practices, while the eventual decline and abandonment of Cahokia implies they were not purely agricultural (which would have sustained them longer).

**Why the others are wrong:**
- **B:** The excerpt explicitly describes Cahokia as a *city* — a fixed, permanent center. "Nomadic" directly contradicts the passage.
- **C:** They left enormous ceremonial mounds — literally the opposite of "very little trace."
- **D:** The excerpt doesn't mention irrigation, and this answer is too specific (irrigation was more characteristic of Southwestern peoples like the Hohokam, not Mississippian cultures).

## Question 3
> (Same excerpt — Kuppermann)
>
> Which of the following most directly contributed to the advanced development of both pre-Columbian American societies described in the excerpt?
>
> A. Creation of military forces stronger than those of most rival societies
> B. Access to waterways to transport goods and trade with other societies
> C. Adaptation to and use of the natural environment for their own benefit
> D. Transfer of power through inheritance, which reinforced spiritual claims to authority

**Notes section:** Unit 1 — Native American Societies Before European Contact

**Key concept:** The question asks what *both* societies — Aztec and Mississippian — shared that drove their advanced development. The common thread in the excerpt is geographic: both were located along or near major waterways that enabled trade and communication.

**Correct answer: B — Access to waterways to transport goods and trade with other societies**
Tenochtitlan was built on an island in Lake Texcoco and relied on canoe trade across the lake and through canal systems. Cahokia was situated "on the Mississippi River near the site of modern St. Louis" — the continent's greatest trade artery. Both cities' size and complexity depended directly on these water-based trade networks.

**Why the others are wrong:**
- **A:** Military strength is mentioned for the Aztecs ("military might") but not for the Mississippians — and it explains *dominance*, not the original development of complexity.
- **C:** Adaptation to environment is true of all human societies and is too vague to explain *advanced* development specifically.
- **D:** Dynastic inheritance is not discussed in the excerpt for either society.

## Question 4
> Questions 4 and 5 refer to the following excerpt:

This one thing is to be done, without which it were in vain to go about this; and that is the matter of planting [colonies] and fortification. . . . We are to plant upon the mouths of the great navigable rivers which are there [in America], by strong order of fortification, and there to plant our colonies. . . . And these fortifications shall keep the [native] people of [America] in obedience and good order. ". . . Without this planting in due time, we shall never be able to have full knowledge of the language, manners, and customs of the people of those regions. . . . > Which of the following developments in the 1500s is best illustrated by the excerpt?
>
> A. European settlers faced resistance from Native Americans.
> B. Europeans transported enslaved Africans to the Americas to produce sugar.

**Key concept:** Hakluyt's *Discourse on Western Planting* is a classic mercantilist argument for English colonization. He frames colonization primarily as an economic project — exporting English goods, importing needed commodities, and securing trade routes before France does.

**Correct answer: C — Europeans sought new sources of wealth in the Americas.**
Hakluyt explicitly identifies the "second chief and principal end" of colonization as economic exchange — selling English cloth and receiving back "needful commodities." The entire document is a commercial argument dressed as imperial strategy.

**Why the others are wrong:**
- **A:** Native resistance is not discussed; the excerpt treats Native peoples as subjects to be "kept in obedience," not as resisters.
- **B:** The slave trade to produce sugar is not mentioned anywhere in this excerpt.
- **D:** Religious conversion is absent — Hakluyt's argument is economic and strategic, not evangelical.

>
> A. Native Americans and Europeans partnered for trade.

> C. Native Americans were sent in large numbers into slavery in Europe.

Not only did very few people of any origin cross the great oceans, but those who did must have been sickly or they would have died on the way, taking their pathogens with them. . . . [Native Americans] were not without their own infections, of course, [but Native Americans] seem to have been without any experience with such Old World maladies as smallpox [and] measles. . . . 'Indications of the susceptibility of [Native Americans] . . . to Old World infections appear almost immediately after the intrusion of the whites. In 1492, Columbus kidnapped a number of [Arawak Indians] to train as interpreters and to show to King Ferdinand and Queen Isabella. Several of them seem to have died on the stormy voyage to Europe [in 1493]. . . . In 1493, Columbus . . . sent 550 [Arawak] slaves . . . off across the Atlantic . . . to be put to work in Spain. The majority of these soon were also dead. . . . What killed the Arawaks in 1493 and 1495? . . . Columbus certainly did not want to kill his interpreters, and slavers and slaveholders have no interest whatever in the outright slaughter of their property . . . The most likely candidates for the role of exterminator of the first [Native Americans] in Europe were those that killed so many other Arawaks in the decades immediately following: Old World pathogens.'"
> — Alfred W. Crosby, historian, *Ecological Imperialism: The Biological Expansion of Europe, 900–1900*, published in 1986
>
> Which of the following developments in the late 1400s and early 1500s is depicted in the excerpt?
>
> A. Native Americans adapted to diverse geographical environments and developed complex societies.
> B. Alliances with Europeans added some Native American societies in their efforts to conquer rival powers.
> C. Europeans persecuted for their religious beliefs established new separatist settlements in the Americas.
> D. Europeans undertook voyages across the Atlantic to the Americas in search of new sources of wealth.

**Notes section:** Unit 1 — Columbian Exchange and European Contact

**Key concept:** Contextualization question — the excerpt describes the initial contact period (1492–1495) as the backdrop for Crosby's disease argument. The broader development depicted is the Atlantic crossings of the late 1400s driven by European economic ambitions.

**Correct answer: D — Europeans undertook voyages across the Atlantic to the Americas in search of new sources of wealth.**
Crosby's excerpt references Columbus's voyages, the transport of enslaved Arawaks to Spain to work, and the general pattern of Atlantic crossing in this era — all driven by the Spanish crown's pursuit of wealth and empire.

**Why the others are wrong:**
- **A:** True historically but not what the excerpt depicts — it's about the *effects* of contact, not Native adaptation before contact.
- **B:** Native alliances with Europeans aren't mentioned here.
- **C:** Religious persecution and separatist settlements (Pilgrims, Puritans) are 17th century — anachronistic for the late 1400s Crosby is discussing.

## Question 10
> Questions 10 through 12 refer to the following excerpt:
> ". . . write an account to Your Majesty as the first [person] to come among these natives . . . "These Indian people of New Spain [Mexico] are vassals of Your Majesty. . . . I dare plead with you for a remedy because, for their people to be saved, they are in great need of relief in order to devote themselves at least somewhat to matters of Faith. After all, it is the struggle for their salvation that justifies their discovery. . . . 'I firmly believe that if the decrees Your Majesty sent here for their benefit were implemented, and if the governors and judges did more than pretend to do so, great good would have come to these people. Even more firmly I believe that Your Majesty's intention is that they should be saved and that they know God. For this to happen, they must have some relief, so that with the moderate labor needed to meet their tribute obligation, they can still give themselves wholeheartedly to our teachings. . . . > Which of the following best describes an argument made by de Gante in the letter?

> B. The emperor should sponsor voyages to discover new American kingdoms.

**Notes section:** Unit 1 — Spanish Colonization and the Encomienda System

**Correct answer: D — The Spanish should require less tribute after conquest to avoid Native American depopulation.**
De Gante's entire letter argues for tribute relief: "they be required to pay tribute only from what they have." He warns that without this change, the Americas will be "deserted as the [Caribbean] islands" — directly connecting excessive tribute to depopulation.

**Why the others are wrong:**

- **B:** De Gante makes no mention of new voyages of discovery.

## Question 13
> In which of the following ways did the Spanish impose racial hierarchies in the regions of the Americas that they controlled during the 1500s and 1600s?
>
> A. The Spanish promoted the status of Native American peoples who supported them to a level in the hierarchy equivalent to the Spanish colonizers.
> B. The Spanish created a caste system that incorporated people of European, Native American, and African descent.
> C. The Spanish avoided using enslaved African laborers and tried to bar them from Spanish colonies.
> D. The Spanish banned interracial marriages and sought to maintain a racially distinct Spanish population as a colonial elite.

**Notes section:** Unit 1 — Spanish Colonization; Spanish Colonial Society

**Key concept:** The *sistema de castas* was a formal racial hierarchy in Spanish colonial America that classified people by the degree of European, Native American, and African ancestry, assigning social status, legal rights, and economic opportunities accordingly.

**Correct answer: B — The Spanish created a caste system that incorporated people of European, Native American, and African descent.**
The *casta* system was the Spanish colonial mechanism for organizing racial hierarchy. Peninsulares (born in Spain) were at the top; then criollos (Spanish descent, born in Americas); then a complex hierarchy of mixed-race categories (mestizo, mulato, etc.); with enslaved Africans and Native Americans at the bottom.

**Why the others are wrong:**
- **A:** Native supporters were never elevated to equal status with Spanish colonizers — the hierarchy was rigid.
- **C:** The opposite — Spanish colonies were a major destination for enslaved Africans, especially in the Caribbean.
- **D:** Interracial relationships were *common* (producing the entire casta classification system) — banning them would have made the casta system unnecessary.

## Question 14
> What was a major difference between the Spanish encomienda system and the Spanish caste system in the Americas?
>
> A. The encomienda system privileged people of Spanish descent, while in the caste system people of indigenous descent had political power.
> B. The encomienda system relied on the support of the Catholic Church, while the caste system was opposed by the Catholic Church.
> C. The encomienda system required inequality, while the caste system assumed social equality.
> D. The encomienda system was based on using Native Americans for forced labor, while the caste system was based on a diverse and racially mixed population.

**Notes section:** Unit 1 — Spanish Colonization and Colonial Labor Systems

**Key concept:** The encomienda was a labor extraction system (Spanish colonizers received the labor/tribute of Native people in a given area); the caste system was a social classification system based on racial mixture. They served different functions and operated on different logics.

**Correct answer: D — The encomienda system was based on using Native Americans for forced labor, while the caste system was based on a diverse and racially mixed population.**
The encomienda specifically extracted Native labor/tribute for Spanish landholders. The caste system, by contrast, emerged from the reality of racial mixing and created a hierarchy that incorporated not just Spanish and Native but also African and mixed-race populations.

**Why the others are wrong:**
- **A:** The caste system did *not* give indigenous people political power — it ranked them below Europeans.
- **B:** The Catholic Church *critiqued* the encomienda (as de Gante's letter shows), not supported it, and the Church wasn't specifically opposed to the caste system either.
- **C:** Both systems required inequality; the caste system was explicitly hierarchical, not egalitarian.

## Question 15
> How were European economic systems in the American colonies in the 1500s and 1600s different from existing economic systems in Europe?
>
> A. Most European colonies were based on agriculture.
> B. French colonists engaged in commercial activities such as the fur trade.
> C. Spanish colonists used enslaved Africans to work on plantations.
> D. Most colonial economies were tightly regulated by the imperial and colonial governments.

**Notes section:** Unit 1 — European Colonization and Mercantilism

**Key concept:** The key economic difference between the colonies and Europe was *mercantilism* — the idea that colonies existed to serve the mother country's economic interests. Colonial economies were far more tightly controlled and directed by imperial regulation than European domestic economies, which had more market-driven elements.

**Correct answer: D — Most colonial economies were tightly regulated by the imperial and colonial governments.**
Mercantilism meant colonies were legally required to trade only with the mother country, produce specific goods, and funnel wealth back to Europe. This degree of regulatory control was stricter than what existed in European domestic markets, making it a genuine *difference*.

**Why the others are wrong:**
- **A:** Europe was also agricultural — this is not a *difference*, just a similarity.
- **B:** French fur trading is a true fact but it's one specific example, not a general difference between colonial and European economies, and the fur trade was also commercially driven just like European trade.
- **C:** Also true, but this describes a labor system, not an economic system difference per se — and it's a feature of *some* colonies, not the general distinction between colonial and European economies.
