## Study Hub Quick Reference — Unit 1

> [!check] MCQ Pattern — Columbian Exchange Framing
> The Columbian Exchange is almost always framed as **bidirectional**: diseases and horses went TO the Americas; foods (corn, potatoes, tomatoes) went TO Europe/Africa. The AP frequently asks about demographic collapse in the Americas — answer: disease, not warfare, killed 50–90% of Native populations.

> [!check] MCQ Pattern — Spanish vs. English Colonial Models
> Spain: centralized crown control, encomienda labor, racial mixing (mestizo society), Catholic missions. England: decentralized, private charters, separate colonies with distinct labor systems. The AP often contrasts these. Don't conflate them.

> [!warning] Trap — Don't Conflate Native Societies
> The AP distinguishes between specific groups: Pueblo peoples (Southwest, settled, agriculture), Mississippian mound-builders (Southeast, chiefdom hierarchy), Iroquois Confederacy (Northeast, matrilineal, political alliance), Plains nomads (horse-centered after Columbian Exchange). Match each society to its region and characteristics.

> [!warning] Trap — "Discovery" Framing
> AP questions often use primary sources that reveal European bias. Recognize Eurocentrism in sources — Columbus's journals, Spanish chronicles — and be able to describe the Native perspective as an alternative interpretation.

## Pre-Columbian Native American Societies

- **Pueblo peoples** (Southwest): sedentary, multi-story stone villages, irrigated agriculture, complex trade networks; Ancestral Puebloans (Anasazi) preceded them
- **Mississippian culture** (Southeast/Midwest): large mound cities (Cahokia, near modern St. Louis, pop. ~10,000–20,000 at peak), hierarchical chiefdoms, corn-based agriculture
- **Eastern Woodlands peoples**: diverse; Algonquian-speaking groups along coast, Iroquois Confederacy (Haudenosaunee) in Northeast — a political alliance of 5 (later 6) nations with matrilineal clans
- **Great Plains**: nomadic before horses; semi-sedentary along river valleys, bison hunting on foot
- **Northwest Coast**: salmon-based economy, totem poles, potlatches (redistribution ceremonies), no agriculture needed
- All societies shaped by geography: coastal fishing, river-valley farming, forest hunting

## European Motivations for Exploration

**The 3 G's:** God (spread Christianity), Glory (national/personal prestige), Gold (wealth through trade routes)

- **Portugal** led early exploration: Prince Henry the Navigator, African coastal routes, reached India 1498 (Vasco da Gama)
- **Spain** backed Columbus (1492): sought western route to Asia; instead reached Caribbean
- Competing motivations pushed exploration: Ottoman control of eastern trade routes (1453 fall of Constantinople) made Atlantic routes valuable
- **Encomienda** authorized Spanish settlers to extract labor from Natives in exchange for "protection" and Christianization

---

## Spanish Colonization

- **Columbus** (1492): reached Caribbean; opened sustained European-American contact
- **Conquistadors**: Cortés defeated Aztecs (1521) using Native allies + disease; Pizarro defeated Inca (1532–1533)
- **Encomienda system**: Spanish crown granted labor rights over Native people to colonists; led to massive exploitation and population collapse
- **Bartolomé de las Casas**: Spanish priest who witnessed encomienda abuses; wrote *A Short Account of the Destruction of the Indies* (1542); advocated for Native rights; his work contributed to the New Laws of 1542 (limiting encomienda) — though rarely enforced
- **Mestizo society**: racial mixing created a Spanish colonial caste system; mestizos (Spanish-Native), mulatos (Spanish-African), etc.
- **Catholic missions**: Franciscan and Jesuit priests established missions throughout the Southwest and Florida; aimed at conversion and labor extraction
- **Columbian Exchange**: disease (smallpox, measles, typhus) killed 50–90% of Native Americas; horses revolutionized Plains societies; crops (corn, potatoes) transformed European nutrition

---

## Early English Exploration and Colonization Attempts

- **John Cabot** (1497): English-backed; explored North American coast; basis for later English land claims
- **Sir Walter Raleigh** and **Roanoke Colony** (1585–1587): first English settlement attempt in North Carolina; the "Lost Colony" — disappeared by 1590, possibly absorbed by Croatoan people or killed by starvation
- English motivations: Protestant rivalry with Catholic Spain, desire for trade routes, opportunity for private profit through joint-stock companies
- **Joint-stock companies**: spread financial risk among investors; key mechanism that would fund Jamestown (1607) just after this unit ends

---

## French Exploration

- Explored interior North America: St. Lawrence River (Cartier, 1534), Great Lakes, Mississippi River (La Salle, 1682)
- Established fur trade alliances with Native peoples — less extractive labor model than Spain
- New France: sparse settlement focused on trade, not plantation agriculture; maintained closer alliances with Natives

## Key Concepts / Acts

| Concept | Significance |
|---|---|

## AP Exam Tips — Unit 1

- Unit 1 is low-weight (4–6%) but appears in **LEQ Era 1 (1491–1800)** and **SAQ Q3 (1491–1877)**
- Common MCQ stimulus: Native American creation accounts or early European explorer journals — you'll be asked to identify perspective or purpose
- Common SAQ/LEQ angle: compare Spanish and English colonial approaches OR explain the demographic impact of European contact on Native Americans
- Know the Columbian Exchange in both directions; AP heavily tests it
- "Contact" framing is key: AP language has shifted away from "discovery" — reflect that in essays
