## Study Hub Quick Reference — Unit 1

> [!check] MCQ Pattern — Columbian Exchange Framing
> The Columbian Exchange is almost always framed as **bidirectional**: diseases and horses went TO the Americas; foods (corn, potatoes, tomatoes) went TO Europe/Africa. The AP frequently asks about demographic collapse in the Americas — answer: disease, not warfare, killed 50–90% of Native populations.

> [!check] MCQ Pattern — Spanish vs. English Colonial Models
> Spain: centralized crown control, encomienda labor, racial mixing (mestizo society), Catholic missions. England: decentralized, private charters, separate colonies with distinct labor systems. The AP often contrasts these. Don't conflate them.

> [!warning] Trap — Don't Conflate Native Societies
> The AP distinguishes between specific groups: Pueblo peoples (Southwest, settled, agriculture), Mississippian mound-builders (Southeast, chiefdom hierarchy), Iroquois Confederacy (Northeast, matrilineal, political alliance), Plains nomads (horse-centered after Columbian Exchange). Match each society to its region and characteristics.

> [!warning] Trap — "Discovery" Framing
> AP questions often use primary sources that reveal European bias. Recognize Eurocentrism in sources — Columbus's journals, Spanish chronicles — and be able to describe the Native perspective as an alternative interpretation.

## Heimler's History — Key Frameworks
**Unit 1 Full Review Video:** [APUSH Unit 1 REVIEW (Period 1: 1491–1607)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jqf_c9Pw8gs)

**Heimler's big idea:** Understand the *diverse* Native societies shaped by their environments, then trace how European arrival changed everything through the Columbian Exchange — with Spain dominating early colonization and establishing systems that set up all future colonial patterns.

### Native Diversity by Region — Heimler's Geography-First Approach
| Region | People | Key Traits |
|---|---|---|
| Southwest (UT/CO) | **Pueblo** | Settled farmers; advanced irrigation; cliff dwellings; hardened clay urban centers |
| Great Basin / Great Plains (CO → Canada) | **Ute** + others | Nomadic; hunter-gatherer; organized into small egalitarian kinship bands |
| California Coast | **Chumash** | Permanent fishing villages (~1,000 people each); regional trade networks up/down the coast |
| Pacific Northwest | **Chinook** | Permanent settlements; extensive plank houses housing entire family kinship groups |
| Northeast | **Iroquois** | Longhouses from abundant timber; matrilineal clans; political Confederacy of 5 (later 6) nations |
| Mississippi River Valley | **Cahokia** | Settled farmers on rich soil; river-based trade; centralized chieftains; pop. 10,000–30,000 |

**Heimler's point:** Native societies were *shaped by their environments* — geography determined economy, which determined society structure. They were not a monolithic group.

### Why Europeans Explored — Heimler's Causal Chain
1. European kingdoms consolidated → wealthy upper class craved Asian luxury goods
2. **Ottoman Empire** controlled land-based routes to Asia (Constantinople fell 1453) → Europeans couldn't trade on their own terms → need for **sea-based routes**
3. **Portugal** moved first — built a trading post empire along Africa; deployed new maritime tech: **astrolabe**, updated astronomical charts, **Latin sail**, **stern post rudder**, smaller/faster ships
4. **Spain** entered after: (a) finished the Reconquista (driving Moorish Muslims from Iberian Peninsula) → fired up to spread Christianity; (b) saw Portugal's wealth → sponsored Columbus (1492)

### Feudalism → Capitalism — Heimler's Economic Shift
- Before New World wealth: **feudalism** — peasants worked nobles' land for protection; limited economic mobility
- Influx of New World gold/silver shifted Europe toward **capitalism** — private ownership, free market exchange
- **Joint stock companies** were the mechanism: pooled investor capital, limited liability (if venture fails, no one loses everything; if succeeds, all share profits) — fundamentally different from state-sponsored exploration like Spain's

### Encomienda Problems → African Slavery — Heimler's Causal Chain
- **Problem 1:** Natives kept escaping — they knew the geography; Spanish did not
- **Problem 2:** Natives kept dying from smallpox
- **Solution:** Import **enslaved Africans** — (1) they had developed immunity to European diseases through centuries of Afro-Eurasian trade contact; (2) they didn't know American geography → less likely to escape successfully
- This is Heimler's causal chain for why African chattel slavery replaced Native encomienda labor

### Casta System — Full Hierarchy
**Peninsulares** (Spanish-born in Spain) → **Criollos/Creoles** (Spanish-born in Americas) → **Castas** (Mestizo = Spanish+Native; Mulato = Spanish+African; and subdivisions) → **Africans** → **Native Americans**

### Theological Debate Over Colonization — Two Competing Views
- **Juan Ginés de Sepúlveda:** Argued Natives were ontologically less than human; they *benefited* from harsh labor conditions → justified brutal encomienda treatment
- **Bartolomé de las Casas:** Spent time among Natives; defended their full humanity; persuaded the king to pass laws ending Native slavery → wealthy nobles lobbied to have those laws repealed, and they were

### Biblical Justification for African Slavery (Ham's Curse)
- Genesis: Noah's son Ham sinned against Noah; Noah cursed Ham's son Canaan and his descendants to be slaves
- European colonizers read Black skin as the "mark of Ham" → Africans were destined for slavery from the beginning by divine decree
- Heimler's note: *That is not what the Bible says.* But that is how they read it — a theological cover for economic exploitation

## Pre-Columbian Native American Societies

- **Pueblo peoples** (Southwest): sedentary, multi-story stone villages, irrigated agriculture, complex trade networks; Ancestral Puebloans (Anasazi) preceded them
- **Mississippian culture** (Southeast/Midwest): large mound cities (Cahokia, near modern St. Louis, pop. ~10,000–20,000 at peak), hierarchical chiefdoms, corn-based agriculture
- **Eastern Woodlands peoples**: diverse; Algonquian-speaking groups along coast, Iroquois Confederacy (Haudenosaunee) in Northeast — a political alliance of 5 (later 6) nations with matrilineal clans
- **Great Plains**: nomadic before horses; semi-sedentary along river valleys, bison hunting on foot
- **Northwest Coast**: salmon-based economy, totem poles, potlatches (redistribution ceremonies), no agriculture needed
- All societies shaped by geography: coastal fishing, river-valley farming, forest hunting

## European Motivations for Exploration

**The 3 G's:** God (spread Christianity), Glory (national/personal prestige), Gold (wealth through trade routes)

- **Portugal** led early exploration: Prince Henry the Navigator, African coastal routes, reached India 1498 (Vasco da Gama)
- **Spain** backed Columbus (1492): sought western route to Asia; instead reached Caribbean
- Competing motivations pushed exploration: Ottoman control of eastern trade routes (1453 fall of Constantinople) made Atlantic routes valuable
- **Encomienda** authorized Spanish settlers to extract labor from Natives in exchange for "protection" and Christianization

## Spanish Colonization

- **Columbus** (1492): reached Caribbean; opened sustained European-American contact
- **Conquistadors**: Cortés defeated Aztecs (1521) using Native allies + disease; Pizarro defeated Inca (1532–1533)
- **Encomienda system**: Spanish crown granted labor rights over Native people to colonists; led to massive exploitation and population collapse
- **Bartolomé de las Casas**: Spanish priest who witnessed encomienda abuses; wrote *A Short Account of the Destruction of the Indies* (1542); advocated for Native rights; his work contributed to the New Laws of 1542 (limiting encomienda) — though rarely enforced
- **Mestizo society**: racial mixing created a Spanish colonial caste system; mestizos (Spanish-Native), mulatos (Spanish-African), etc.
- **Catholic missions**: Franciscan and Jesuit priests established missions throughout the Southwest and Florida; aimed at conversion and labor extraction
- **Columbian Exchange**: disease (smallpox, measles, typhus) killed 50–90% of Native Americas; horses revolutionized Plains societies; crops (corn, potatoes) transformed European nutrition

---

## Early English Exploration and Colonization Attempts

- **John Cabot** (1497): English-backed; explored North American coast; basis for later English land claims
- **Sir Walter Raleigh** and **Roanoke Colony** (1585–1587): first English settlement attempt in North Carolina; the "Lost Colony" — disappeared by 1590, possibly absorbed by Croatoan people or killed by starvation
- English motivations: Protestant rivalry with Catholic Spain, desire for trade routes, opportunity for private profit through joint-stock companies
- **Joint-stock companies**: spread financial risk among investors; key mechanism that would fund Jamestown (1607) just after this unit ends

---

## French Exploration

- Explored interior North America: St. Lawrence River (Cartier, 1534), Great Lakes, Mississippi River (La Salle, 1682)
- Established fur trade alliances with Native peoples — less extractive labor model than Spain
- New France: sparse settlement focused on trade, not plantation agriculture; maintained closer alliances with Natives

### FRQ Part A — SAQ

**Excerpts used:** Isabel de Guevara (wife of conquistador, petition 1556) + Chief of Acuera (response to de Soto, 1539)

**(a) Describe ONE claim made in the excerpt. [1 pt]**
Pick ONE from either source:
- **De Guevara:** Women made essential military/logistical contributions (nursing, standing guard, loading crossbows) equal to or exceeding men's — yet were excluded from encomienda grants that rewarded male colonizers.
- **Chief of Acuera:** Submitting to Spanish authority would dishonor him as a free king; Native leaders had both the right and the will to resist Spanish demands militarily.

**(b) Describe ONE historical development illustrated by the excerpts. [1 pt]**
Pick ONE:
- The Chief's defiance illustrates **Native American resistance to Spanish colonization** — the pattern of Native leaders actively resisting demands for submission using military force and assertions of sovereignty.
- De Guevara illustrates **the encomienda as the mechanism for distributing colonial rewards** — and the gendered exclusion that left women without formal economic recognition despite their contributions to conquest.

**Total: 2 pts**

### FRQ Part B — LEQ

**Prompt:** *Evaluate the reasons why European powers sought to establish colonies in the Americas in the period from 1492 to 1607.*

**"Evaluate" = take a position on WHICH reasons mattered most and HOW they interacted.**

**Strong thesis:** Economic motives were primary, but intertwined with religious goals and inter-European strategic competition that made colonization about national prestige and security as well as profit.

**Evidence to use:**
| Category | Specific Evidence |
|---|---|
| Economic | Spain's encomienda extracting silver/gold; Hakluyt's *Discourse* framing colonization as commercial enterprise; Potosí silver reshaping European economy |
| Religious | *Requerimiento*; de Gante's letter showing religious justification provided moral cover for economic extraction; Protestant vs. Catholic rivalry added urgency |
| Inter-European competition | Hakluyt warning France would "secretly fortify and settle" first; Treaty of Tordesillas (1494) as evidence of early formalized competition over the non-European world |

**Context to establish:** Ottoman control of overland Asian trade routes (Constantinople fell 1453) → forced Atlantic exploration; Portugal pioneered the way → Spain followed.

**Scoring:** Thesis (1) + Context (1) + Two specific evidence (2) = **4 pts total**

**Weak thesis to avoid:** "Europeans colonized for economic, religious, and political reasons." → That's a list, not a line of reasoning. Take a position on which mattered most or how they interacted.
