# APUSH — Unit 2 Notes: Colonial America (1607–1754)
**Exam Weight: 6–8% | Heimler's History: search "Heimler's History APUSH Unit 2" on YouTube**

---

## Study Hub Quick Reference — Unit 2

> [!check] MCQ Pattern — Chesapeake vs. New England
> AP constantly contrasts these two regions. Chesapeake: tobacco economy, indentured servants → enslaved labor, mortality-driven instability, large plantations, cash-crop monoculture, Church of England. New England: Puritan religious community, town meetings, subsistence farming + fishing + trade, longer lives, more stable families. Know both cold.

> [!check] MCQ Pattern — Bacon's Rebellion → Shift to Slavery
> Bacon's Rebellion (1676) is a pivotal cause the AP loves. Frustrated indentured servants (poor whites) joined Nathaniel Bacon against the colonial elite. After the rebellion, planters shifted to enslaved African labor — enslaved people couldn't vote, organize, or gain freedom after a term. It directly accelerated the slave codes.

> [!warning] Trap — Puritans Were Not Tolerant
> "Puritans fled persecution and supported religious freedom" is wrong. They wanted freedom *for themselves*, not for others. Massachusetts Bay expelled Roger Williams (→ Rhode Island) and Anne Hutchinson for theological dissent. The colony enforced religious conformity.

> [!warning] Trap — Salutary Neglect Is Not Unit 3
> Salutary neglect (Britain's policy of loosely enforcing trade laws, leaving colonies largely self-governing) begins in this period. Its end after 1763 is what causes Unit 3's imperial crisis. Understand it here to explain the Unit 3 reaction.

## Chesapeake Colonies (Virginia and Maryland)

- **Jamestown** (1607): first permanent English settlement; Virginia Company joint-stock venture; early years catastrophic (Starving Time, 1609–10)
- **Tobacco economy**: John Rolfe introduced marketable tobacco (1612); transformed Virginia from struggling settlement to profitable colony
- **Headright system**: 50 acres per person brought to Virginia → incentivized importing laborers; initially filled with **indentured servants** from England
- **Indentured servants**: contracted labor for 4–7 years in exchange for passage; upon freedom, received land, tools, clothes — but little land was available by mid-1600s → growing class of resentful poor whites
- **Bacon's Rebellion (1676)**: Nathaniel Bacon led poor farmers and freed servants against Governor Berkeley's government; attacked Native villages and burned Jamestown; Bacon died of dysentery, rebellion collapsed, but its warning was heard — planters began shifting to enslaved African labor
- **African slavery**: began arriving 1619 (first recorded Africans in Virginia); initially some had quasi-servant status; **slave codes** (1660s–1680s) formalized permanent, hereditary, race-based slavery
- Maryland: founded 1632 by Lord Baltimore (Catholic); **Act of Toleration (1649)** protected all Christians — but later revoked

---

## New England Colonies

- **Separatists/Pilgrims**: Plymouth Colony (1620); Mayflower Compact — early self-governance precedent
- **Puritans**: Massachusetts Bay Colony (1630); John Winthrop's "city upon a hill" sermon; theocratic government; General Court (elected legislature) — early representative body
- **Dissenters expelled**:
  - **Roger Williams**: challenged Puritan land rights and separation of church/state → founded Providence, Rhode Island (1636); earliest colony with religious tolerance
  - **Anne Hutchinson**: antinomianism (grace alone, not deeds); challenged male clergy authority → expelled 1638, went to Rhode Island
- **Halfway Covenant (1662)**: allowed partial church membership for unconverted children of members; reflected declining religious intensity (jeremiad tradition)
- **King Philip's War (1675–76)**: Wampanoag leader Metacom (King Philip) led war against English settlements; bloodiest per capita conflict in American history; ended with Native defeat and collapse of Native power in New England
- Economy: subsistence farming, fishing, timber, trade (triangular trade routes); no dominant cash crop

---

## Middle Colonies

- New York (former Dutch New Netherland, seized 1664), New Jersey, Pennsylvania, Delaware
- **Pennsylvania**: founded by **William Penn** (Quaker, 1681); advertised religious tolerance; attracted diverse European immigrants (Germans, Scots-Irish, Dutch)
- **Quakers**: believed in inner light of God in all people; pacifists; opposed slavery (first organized antislavery protests in America)
- Most ethnically and religiously diverse region; also most economically diverse (grain farming, trade, crafts)

---

## Southern Colonies

- **Carolina** (split into North/South Carolina, 1712) and **Georgia** (1733, founded by James Oglethorpe as buffer against Spanish Florida and refuge for debtors — though debtor-haven plan quickly abandoned)
- South Carolina: rice and indigo economy; heavily enslaved workforce; slave majority by 1708; **Stono Rebellion (1739)** — largest slave uprising in colonial America; led to harsher slave codes
- Georgia: initially banned slavery; reversed by 1750

---

## The Development of Slavery

- Triangular trade: New England rum → Africa (traded for enslaved people) → Caribbean/colonies (traded for molasses) → New England
- **Middle Passage**: horrific transatlantic crossing; 10–15% mortality; enslaved people packed in inhumane conditions
- African cultural retention: enslaved people maintained languages, religions (syncretized with Christianity), music, family structures despite attempts to destroy them
- **Gullah-Geechee culture** (South Carolina, Georgia): preserved African linguistic and cultural elements most completely due to geographic isolation
- Slave codes created permanent, hereditary, race-based slavery: status followed the mother (partus sequitur ventrem)

---

## Colonial Society and Culture

- **Great Awakening (1730s–1740s)**: evangelical religious revival; preachers like **Jonathan Edwards** ("Sinners in the Hands of an Angry God") and **George Whitefield**; challenged established church authority; democratized religion; first truly inter-colonial cultural event
- **Enlightenment**: rationalist philosophy from Europe; Benjamin Franklin embodied American Enlightenment — practical science, self-improvement, reason; influenced founders' political thinking
- **Colonial assemblies**: each colony developed a representative legislature; by 1750, assemblies controlled taxation and spending; set precedent for self-governance
- **Salutary neglect**: British policy of loosely enforcing trade laws (Navigation Acts); colonies grew accustomed to self-governance; when Britain ended this policy after 1763, conflict followed

## AP Exam Tips — Unit 2

- **Bacon's Rebellion → shift to slavery** is one of the most commonly tested causal chains in APUSH; know it cold
- **Great Awakening** often appears in MCQ stimulus (sermon excerpts); recognize democratic/anti-authority themes
- Unit 2 is part of **LEQ Era 1 (1491–1800)**; common prompt: "Evaluate the extent to which the experiences of different colonial regions shaped distinct regional identities"
- **SAQ Q3** may ask you to compare colonial regions or explain the development of slavery
- The AP distinguishes between Separatists (Pilgrims, Plymouth) and Puritans (Massachusetts Bay) — don't conflate them
- Know the **Triangular Trade** flow: it tests on maps and as a causation question for the spread of slavery
