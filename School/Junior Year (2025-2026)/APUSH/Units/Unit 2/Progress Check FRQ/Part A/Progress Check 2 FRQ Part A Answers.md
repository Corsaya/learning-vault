## FRQ Type: SAQ (Short Answer Question) × 2

## SAQ 1

**Source:** John Hanbury, London merchant, and colonial Virginian investors, petition to King George II of Great Britain, delivered to the king's ministers, 1748

**Excerpt:**
> "Your petitioners beg leave humbly to inform your Majesty that the lands to the west of the [Appalachian] mountains, are extremely fertile, the climate very fine and healthy, and the waters of the Mississippi [River], and those of the Potomack [River in Virginia], are only separated by one small ridge of mountains. . . . British goods may be carried at little expense, and afforded reasonably to the Indians in those parts, [if] the lands to the west of the said mountains were settled, and a fort erected in some proper place there. . . . if your petitioners meet with that success they have the greatest reason to expect, it will . . . extend your Majesty's empire in America . . . and, in a short space of time very considerably increase your Majesty's revenue. . . .
>
> Your petitioners, for these great and national ends and purposes . . . have entered into partnership, by the name of the Ohio company, to settle these [lands] to the west of the said mountains. . . . Your petitioners . . . most humbly pray that your Majesty will be graciously pleased to encourage this their said undertaking, by giving instructions to your governor of Virginia to grant to your petitioners a tract . . . of 500,000 acres of land."

---

### Part A
**Task:** Briefly identify ONE cause of the development depicted in the petition.

**Full answer:**
One cause of the Ohio Company's petition was the end of the War of Austrian Succession (1748), which temporarily resolved Anglo-French conflict in Europe and encouraged British merchants and colonists to press for westward expansion into the Ohio Valley before France could consolidate its presence there. More broadly, the petition reflects the persistent British desire to control the fur trade and land west of the Appalachians, driven by the commercial interests of London merchants like Hanbury and Virginia land speculators who saw the Ohio Valley's agricultural and trade potential as enormously profitable if settlement could be secured.

**To earn full credit:** Name ONE specific cause — economic, political, or strategic — and connect it to the petition's content. One sentence with a specific causal link earns the point.

---

### Part B
**Task:** Briefly describe ONE argument made in the petition.

**Full answer:**
The Ohio Company argues that establishing British settlements west of the Appalachians and building a fort in the region would extend the king's empire, increase royal revenue, and allow British goods to be traded advantageously with Native peoples in the interior — framing private land speculation as a service to the British Crown and a means of projecting imperial power deeper into North America.

**To earn full credit:** Identify a specific argument from the text — not just "they wanted land" but *why they argued they deserved it* or *what they claimed would result*. The petition makes both a commercial argument (profit, revenue) and an imperial one (extend the empire, undercut French influence).

---

### Part C
**Task:** Briefly describe ONE similarity between the British-Native American relations described in the petition and French-Native American relations.

**Full answer:**
Both British and French colonizers used trade relationships with Native Americans as a tool of imperial expansion: the Ohio Company petition explicitly references supplying "Indians in those parts" with British goods at low cost as part of its strategy, just as French traders and officials used the fur trade to maintain alliances with Native peoples across the interior. In both cases, commercial exchange with Native Americans served colonial imperial goals — securing loyalty, establishing presence, and undermining rival European powers — rather than being pursued purely for mutual benefit.

**To earn full credit:** Identify ONE genuine parallel between British and French Native relations. Both used trade diplomatically; both sought Native alliances against European rivals; both needed Native cooperation for interior access. Any of these earns the point if stated specifically.

## SAQ 2

**Source:** John Winthrop, future governor of the Massachusetts Bay Colony, "General Considerations for the Plantation in New England," 1629

**Excerpt:**
> "What warrant [right] have we to take that land, which is and hath been of long time possessed by others . . . ?
>
> That which is common to all is proper to none. [Native Americans] ruleth over many lands without title or property: for they enclose [fence in] no ground, neither have they cattle to maintain it. . . . And why may not Christians have liberty to go and dwell amongst them in their waste[d] lands and woods (leaving them such places as they have [fertilized] for their corn) . . . ? For God hath given to the sons of men a twofold right to the earth; there is a natural right and a civil [political] right. The first right was natural when men held the earth in common, every man sowing and feeding where he pleased. Then, as men and cattle increased, they appropriated some parcels of ground by enclosing [them as property] . . . and this in time got them a civil right."

---

### Part A
**Task:** Briefly identify ONE historical situation in which the excerpt was produced.

**Full answer:**
Winthrop wrote this document in 1629, on the eve of the Puritan Great Migration to Massachusetts Bay Colony (1630–1640), during a period when English Puritans faced increasing religious and political pressure under King Charles I and Archbishop Laud's efforts to enforce Anglican conformity. The excerpt was produced as part of Winthrop's preparation for justifying the Puritan settlement of New England — specifically, anticipating the objection that English colonists had no legal or moral right to occupy land already inhabited by Native Americans.

**To earn full credit:** Identify the specific historical context — the Puritan Great Migration, Anglican persecution of Puritans, or the founding of Massachusetts Bay Colony — and connect it to why Winthrop was writing this document.

---

### Part B
**Task:** Briefly describe ONE argument made in the excerpt.

**Full answer:**
Winthrop argues that because Native Americans did not enclose, fence, or cultivate the land in the way that English agricultural society defined property ownership, they had only a "natural right" (common use) rather than a "civil right" (legal title) to the land — and therefore English colonists who would improve and enclose the land could legitimately settle it without violating Native peoples' legal rights. This argument used English legal concepts of property to redefine Native land use as non-ownership, transforming dispossession into a legally and morally neutral act.

**To earn full credit:** Describe one specific argument, not just "he thought they could take the land." The key argument is the distinction between "natural right" (unimproved common use) and "civil right" (legally constituted property through enclosure), and how this distinction justified English colonization.

---

### Part C
**Task:** Briefly identify ONE historical effect of the development described in the excerpt.

**Full answer:**
One historical effect of the English legal justification for taking Native land — as articulated by Winthrop and similar colonial ideologues — was the dispossession of coastal New England Native peoples from their territories over the following decades, culminating in King Philip's War (1675–1676), in which the Wampanoag, Nipmuc, and Narragansett peoples mounted a major armed resistance against English expansion. The conflict resulted in the near-destruction of Native political power in southern New England and accelerated English territorial control over the entire region — the direct long-term consequence of the colonial land philosophy Winthrop articulated in 1629.

**To earn full credit:** Identify a specific historical effect — either the dispossession of Native peoples, King Philip's War, or the broader pattern of colonial land seizure — and connect it causally to the ideological development Winthrop represents.
