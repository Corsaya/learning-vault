## Study Hub Quick Reference — Unit 8

> [!check] MCQ Pattern — Cold War Policy Documents
> AP constantly uses NSC-68, Truman Doctrine, Marshall Plan excerpts as stimuli. Identify the containment logic: Soviets are expansionist → U.S. must contain them wherever they try to expand → justifies military buildup, foreign aid, and intervention. This framework drives U.S. foreign policy from 1947 to 1991.

> [!check] MCQ Pattern — Civil Rights Movement Sequence
> Know the chronology: *Brown v. Board* (1954) → Montgomery Bus Boycott (1955–56) → sit-ins (1960) → Freedom Rides (1961) → Birmingham (1963) → March on Washington (1963) → Civil Rights Act (1964) → Voting Rights Act (1965) → Black Power (mid-1960s) → Fair Housing Act (1968). AP tests both the events AND the legislation they produced.

> [!warning] Trap — Second Red Scare vs. First Red Scare
> First Red Scare (1919–20): Palmer Raids, labor unions as communist threat. **Second Red Scare (1947–57)**: HUAC, Hollywood Ten, McCarthy's Senate investigations. Don't conflate them. The Second Red Scare is the context for McCarthyism — which the AP often tests with primary source excerpts.

> [!warning] Trap — Vietnam War Complexity
> The AP expects nuance: U.S. intervened based on Domino Theory / containment, but also Cold War credibility concerns. The war divided American society. Escalation under LBJ (Gulf of Tonkin Resolution → massive troop buildup) contrasts with Nixon's Vietnamization and withdrawal. Know why the U.S. entered, escalated, and eventually withdrew.

> [!danger] Critical — Great Society vs. New Deal
> MCQ and LEQ often ask students to compare these. Both expanded federal power, but: New Deal focused on economic recovery (depression context). Great Society focused on equality and poverty (prosperity context): Civil Rights Acts, Voting Rights Act, Medicare, Medicaid, Head Start, federal education funding. Know specific programs for each.

## Cold War Origins (1945–1950)

**Post-WWII World:**
- U.S. and USSR emerged as superpowers; former allies now rivals
- USSR occupied Eastern Europe; imposed communist governments (Soviet satellites)
- Atomic monopoly: U.S. had it (1945); USSR developed it (1949) → nuclear arms race

**Containment Policy:**
- **George Kennan's "Long Telegram" (1946)**: Soviet expansion is inevitable tendency; U.S. must contain it through patient, firm counter-pressure
- **Iron Curtain speech (Churchill, 1946)**: announced division of Europe
- **Truman Doctrine (1947)**: U.S. would support free peoples resisting communist subjugation; initially for Greece and Turkey; became universal principle
- **Marshall Plan (1948)**: $13 billion U.S. aid to rebuild Western Europe; prevent economic desperation from pushing countries toward communism; enormously successful
- **Berlin Blockade and Airlift (1948–49)**: Soviets blockaded West Berlin; U.S. and Allies airlifted supplies for 11 months; Soviets backed down
- **NATO (1949)**: North Atlantic Treaty Organization; collective defense pact ("an attack on one is an attack on all"); first U.S. peacetime military alliance

**NSC-68 (1950):**
- Top-secret policy document; recommended quadrupling defense spending
- Framed Cold War as existential struggle; justified massive military buildup
- Justified by: Soviet atomic bomb (1949), fall of China to Mao's communists (1949)

## Korea and the Limits of Containment (1950–1953)

- **Korean War (1950–53)**: North Korea (Soviet-backed) invaded South Korea; Truman sent troops under UN mandate without Congressional declaration of war
- General MacArthur: pushed North Koreans to Chinese border → China intervened; MacArthur publicly demanded war on China → Truman fired him for insubordination; civilian control of military asserted
- **Stalemate at 38th parallel**; armistice 1953; no peace treaty to this day; 54,000 Americans died
- "Limited war" concept: nuclear weapons made total war impossible; fight to contain, not win

---

## Second Red Scare and McCarthyism (1947–1957)

- **HUAC (House Un-American Activities Committee)**: investigated communist infiltration; Hollywood Ten imprisoned for refusing to testify (1st Amendment); blacklists destroyed careers
- **Alger Hiss**: State Department official; convicted of perjury re: espionage; convinced many that communist spies were real
- **Rosenbergs (1953)**: Julius and Ethel Rosenberg executed for passing atomic secrets to Soviets — most controversial execution in U.S. history
- **Joseph McCarthy**: Wisconsin senator; claimed to have lists of communists in State Department; no evidence but ruined hundreds of careers; "McCarthyism" = reckless accusation without evidence
- **Army-McCarthy Hearings (1954)**: televised; attorney Joseph Welch: "Have you no sense of decency?" — McCarthy's support collapsed; censured by Senate
- **Levittown Effect**: prosperity + conformity; fear of communism made dissent dangerous

---

## 1950s: Prosperity, Suburbia, and Conformity

**The Postwar Boom:**
- GI Bill (1944): veterans' mortgages, education benefits → college boom, suburban homeownership
- **Baby Boom**: 1946–64 birth cohort of 76 million; drove consumer economy
- **Suburbanization**: Levittown (NY and PA); mass-produced suburbs; car-dependent; highways (Interstate Highway Act, 1956 — largest public works project in history)
- Consumer culture: television, refrigerators, cars, credit cards; "keeping up with the Joneses"
- **Television**: by 1960, 90% of homes had TV; transformed politics, culture, news

**Conformity and Critique:**
- Conformity pressure; corporate "organization man"; cookie-cutter suburbs
- **Beats (Beat Generation)**: Jack Kerouac (*On the Road*), Allen Ginsberg (*Howl*); rejected conformity, celebrated nonconformity, jazz, travel
- Rock and roll emerged; threatened racial and generational boundaries (Elvis)

**Eisenhower Presidency:**
- "Hidden hand" leadership; modern Republicanism; accepted New Deal's welfare state
- **Military-Industrial Complex warning (1961 farewell address)**: danger of defense industry's political influence
- Interstate Highway Act (1956): 41,000 miles of highways; transformed transportation; ostensibly for military evacuation but transformed suburbs, trucking, American life

## The Civil Rights Movement (1954–1968)

**Legal Groundwork:**
- **NAACP legal strategy**: Thurgood Marshall argued Brown; decades of chipping away at Plessy
- ***Brown v. Board of Education* (1954)**: Supreme Court unanimously overturned Plessy; "separate but equal" is inherently unequal; Thurgood Marshall argued for NAACP
- "Southern Manifesto" (1956): 101 Southern congressmen vowed to resist Brown; "massive resistance"
- **Little Rock Crisis (1957)**: Arkansas Governor Faubus blocked integration; Eisenhower sent 101st Airborne to enforce it; federal supremacy asserted

**Nonviolent Direct Action (SCLC Strategy):**
- **Montgomery Bus Boycott (1955–56)**: Rosa Parks arrested for refusing to give up seat; 381-day boycott; Supreme Court ruled bus segregation unconstitutional; launched MLK's career
- **Greensboro sit-ins (1960)**: Black students sat at Woolworth's lunch counter; SNCC (Student Nonviolent Coordinating Committee) formed
- **Freedom Rides (1961)**: interracial activists rode buses into Deep South; violently attacked; Kennedy forced to enforce desegregation of interstate transportation
- **Birmingham Campaign (1963)**: Bull Connor used fire hoses and police dogs on peaceful marchers; televised nationally; shocked the country; pushed Kennedy to propose Civil Rights Act
- **March on Washington (August 28, 1963)**: 250,000 people; MLK's "I Have a Dream" speech; pressured Congress
- **Civil Rights Act (1964)**: banned discrimination in public accommodations; prohibited employment discrimination; established EEOC; most comprehensive civil rights legislation since Reconstruction
- **Selma to Montgomery March (1965)** / "Bloody Sunday": police brutality on Edmund Pettus Bridge; televised; pushed Congress to pass:
- **Voting Rights Act (1965)**: banned discriminatory voting tests; federal registrars in South; Black voter registration surged

**Beyond the South — Black Power:**
- Northern de facto segregation (housing, schools, economic inequality) not addressed by Civil Rights Act
- **Malcolm X**: Black nationalism; self-defense; "by any means necessary"; Nation of Islam → later moderated; assassinated 1965
- **Black Power (Stokely Carmichael, 1966)**: Black political and economic self-determination; rejected integration as a goal
- **Black Panther Party (1966)**: Oakland; community programs (breakfast, medical clinics) + armed patrols against police brutality
- **Urban riots**: Watts (1965), Detroit (1967), Newark — reflected frustration with slow progress and economic inequality; Kerner Commission: "two societies, one Black, one white — separate and unequal"
- **MLK assassinated (April 4, 1968)**; **Fair Housing Act (1968)**: banned housing discrimination

## LBJ and the Great Society (1964–1968)

**Landslide Election (1964):** LBJ vs. Goldwater; 61% of popular vote; large congressional majority → legislative window

**Great Society Programs:**
| Program | Year | What It Did |
|---|---|---|
| Medicare | 1965 | Federal health insurance for Americans 65+ |
| Medicaid | 1965 | Federal health insurance for low-income Americans |
| Elementary and Secondary Education Act | 1965 | First major federal education funding |
| Head Start | 1965 | Early childhood education for low-income children |
| Economic Opportunity Act (War on Poverty) | 1964 | Job training, community action programs |
| Immigration and Nationality Act | 1965 | Abolished national origins quotas; prioritized family reunification; transformed immigration patterns |
| Voting Rights Act | 1965 | Banned discriminatory voting practices |
| Fair Housing Act | 1968 | Banned housing discrimination |

**Legacy:** Poverty rate fell from 19% (1964) to 12% (1969); Medicare/Medicaid created lasting safety net; but Great Society was undermined by costs of Vietnam War

## The Vietnam War (1955–1975)

**Background:**
- Vietnam divided at 17th parallel (1954) after French defeat at Dien Bien Phu; Ho Chi Minh led communist North; Diem led U.S.-backed South
- **Domino Theory**: if one country fell to communism, neighbors would follow
- U.S. supported South with advisors, money, equipment; Eisenhower → Kennedy increased commitment

**Escalation (LBJ, 1964–68):**
- **Gulf of Tonkin Resolution (1964)**: Congress authorized president to use military force in Southeast Asia; based on disputed attack on U.S. ships; gave LBJ blank check; massive troop buildup (500,000 troops by 1968)
- **Search and destroy missions**; napalm; Agent Orange defoliant; strategic hamlets
- **Tet Offensive (January 1968)**: Viet Cong and NVA simultaneous attacks on 100 South Vietnamese cities; militarily repelled but proved U.S. was NOT winning as claimed; shattered public trust; Walter Cronkite: "we are mired in stalemate"; LBJ declined to run for re-election

**Anti-War Movement:**
- Draft resistance (burning draft cards); student protests (Students for a Democratic Society, SDS)
- **Kent State (1970)**: National Guard killed 4 student protesters at Kent State University, Ohio; galvanized anti-war movement
- Vietnam Veterans Against the War; Congressional hearings on atrocities (My Lai Massacre, 1968)

**Nixon and Vietnamization (1969–75):**
- **Vietnamization**: withdraw U.S. troops; train South Vietnamese to fight
- Secretly bombed Cambodia (1969–70); invaded Cambodia (1970) → Kent State backlash
- **Paris Peace Accords (1973)**: ceasefire; U.S. withdrawal; prisoners of war returned
- **War Powers Act (1973)**: Congress must authorize military deployments within 60 days; reaction to executive overreach
- Saigon fell **April 30, 1975** — South Vietnam conquered; 58,000 Americans dead; 2–3 million Vietnamese dead

---

## Nixon, Détente, and Watergate (1969–1974)

**Foreign Policy (Nixon + Kissinger):**
- **Détente**: relaxation of Cold War tensions; pragmatic coexistence with Soviets
- **SALT I (1972)**: Strategic Arms Limitation Treaty; first nuclear arms control agreement
- **Opening to China (1972)**: Nixon visited China; recognized People's Republic; split communist bloc

**Watergate (1972–74):**
- June 1972: operatives connected to Nixon's re-election committee broke into Democratic National Committee headquarters at Watergate Hotel
- Nixon covered up White House involvement; "I am not a crook"
- **Saturday Night Massacre (1973)**: Nixon ordered firing of special prosecutor Cox; Attorney General Richardson and deputy resigned rather than comply
- White House tapes subpoenaed; Supreme Court ruled 8-0 Nixon must turn over tapes (*U.S. v. Nixon*)
- Nixon resigned **August 9, 1974** — only president to resign; Gerald Ford pardoned him

---

## 1970s: Limits and Malaise

- **OPEC Oil Embargo (1973)**: Arab oil producers cut off oil to U.S. in retaliation for Israel support; gas lines; energy crisis
- **Stagflation**: simultaneous inflation + stagnation + unemployment; defied Keynesian economics; Nixon imposed wage-price controls (failed)
- **Environmental Movement**: Rachel Carson's *Silent Spring* (1962) launched it; **Earth Day (1970)**; EPA created (1970); Clean Air Act, Clean Water Act
- **Second-wave Feminism**: Betty Friedan's *The Feminine Mystique* (1963); NOW (1966); Title IX (1972, banned sex discrimination in education); ERA passed Congress but not ratified; *Roe v. Wade* (1973) — abortion rights as privacy right
- **Carter (1977–81)**: Camp David Accords (Egypt-Israel peace); Iranian hostage crisis (1979–81); energy crisis; stagflation; "malaise speech"

## AP Exam Tips — Unit 8

- **Most heavily DBQ-tested unit**: civil rights, Cold War, Vietnam — all richly documented periods
- **LEQ Era 3 (1890–2001)**: "Evaluate the extent to which the Civil Rights Movement transformed American society" OR "Evaluate the causes of the U.S. involvement in Vietnam"
- **SAQ Q1 and Q2 are required for 1754–1980** — this unit is directly in scope for BOTH required SAQs
- **Continuity thread**: from New Deal → Great Society → modern welfare state; from Plessy → *Brown* → Civil Rights Act → Voting Rights Act
- **Contrast**: Cold War foreign interventionism vs. domestic civil rights failures — contradiction between fighting communism "for freedom" while denying rights at home is a classic AP complexity point
- Know **both** MLK and Malcolm X as distinct responses to the same problem — AP frequently asks you to compare
- Watergate's significance: permanent damage to trust in government; explains 1970s cynicism
