**[[Unit 1 Notes]] | [[Unit 1 Examples]] | [[Unit 1 Progress Check]]**

> [!note] Unit 1 Key Traps
> - PES: higher binding energy = closer to nucleus (core), lower = valence
> - Atomic radius **increases** going down a group and **decreases** going left→right
> - Molar mass in g/mol; atomic mass in amu — never swap units

### Unit 5 — Kinetics (7–9%)
**[[Unit 5 Notes]] | [[Unit 5 Examples]] | [[Unit 5 Progress Check]]**

| Topic | What to Know |
|---|---|
| 5.1 Reaction Rates | Rate = −Δ[A]/Δt / coefficient; stoichiometric rate ratios |
| 5.2 Rate Laws | $\text{rate} = k[\text{A}]^m[\text{B}]^n$; orders from data tables |
| 5.3 Integrated Rate Laws | Zero (linear [A]), 1st (linear ln[A]), 2nd (linear 1/[A]); half-life |
| 5.4 Elementary Reactions | Molecularity = order for elementary steps only |
| 5.5 Collision Model | Sufficient energy AND proper orientation required |
| 5.6 Energy Profiles | Eₐ, transition state, ΔH from diagram |
| 5.7–5.8 Mechanisms & Rate Law | Rate law from slow step; intermediates cancel |
| 5.9 Steady-State | [intermediate] ≈ constant; derive rate law algebraically |
| 5.10 Multistep Energy Profiles | Identify steps, intermediates, activated complexes |
| 5.11 Catalysis & Arrhenius | $k = Ae^{-E_a/RT}$; catalyst lowers Eₐ, increases k |

> [!note] Unit 5 Key Traps
> - Rate law comes from experiments, not the overall balanced equation
> - Integrated rate law: plot the one that gives a **straight line** to ID order
> - Half-life for 1st order only is constant: $t_{1/2} = 0.693/k$

### Unit 7 — Equilibrium (7–9%)
**[[Unit 7 Notes]] | [[Unit 7 Examples]] | [[Unit 7 Progress Check]]**

| Topic | What to Know |
|---|---|
| 7.1 Intro to Equilibrium | Dynamic equilibrium: forward rate = reverse rate |
| 7.2 Direction of Reversible Rxns | Compare Q vs. K to predict shift direction |
| 7.3 Keq Expression | Products over reactants (molar coefficients as exponents); omit solids/pure liquids |
| 7.4 Calculating Keq | From equilibrium concentrations |
| 7.5–7.6 Magnitude & Properties | K >> 1 → products favored; Kc vs. Kp; Knet = K₁ × K₂ |
| 7.7 ICE Tables | Initial → Change → Equilibrium; solve for x |
| 7.8 Representations | Particle diagrams at equilibrium |
| 7.9–7.10 Le Chatelier's | Add/remove species, change P/V/T → predict shift |
| 7.11 KSP | $K_{sp} = [\text{ions}]^{\text{coefficients}}$; molar solubility from KSP |
| 7.12 Common-Ion Effect | Adding common ion suppresses solubility |

> [!note] Unit 7 Key Traps
> - When Q < K → shift right (toward products) to reach equilibrium
> - Adding inert gas at constant V: no shift (concentrations unchanged)
> - Kp = Kc(RT)^Δn — only needed when Δn ≠ 0 and converting between them

### Unit 9 — Thermodynamics & Electrochemistry (7–9%)
**[[Unit 9 Notes]] | [[Unit 9 Examples]] | [[Unit 9 Progress Check]]**

| Topic | What to Know |
|---|---|
| 9.1 Entropy | S increases: gas production, dissolution, heating; entropy hierarchy |
| 9.2 Calculating ΔS° | $\Delta S° = \sum S°_{prod} - \sum S°_{react}$; S° ≠ 0 for elements |
| 9.3 Gibbs Free Energy | $\Delta G° = \Delta H° - T\Delta S°$; ΔG° < 0 → spontaneous |
| 9.4–9.5 Equilibrium & Kinetics | ΔG° < 0 → K > 1; kinetic control when Eₐ is large |
| 9.6 Free Energy of Dissolution | ΔG of dissolving; entropy vs. enthalpy competition |
| 9.7 Coupled Reactions | Add ΔG values; unfavorable driven by favorable |
| 9.8 Galvanic/Electrolytic Cells | Anode = oxidation, cathode = reduction; salt bridge |
| 9.9 Cell Potential & ΔG | $\Delta G° = -nFE°$; positive E°cell → spontaneous |
| 9.10 Nernst Equation | $E = E° - (RT/nF)\ln Q$ or $E° - (0.0592/n)\log Q$ at 25°C |
| 9.11 Faraday's Law | $q = nF$; moles of electrons = current × time / F |

> [!note] Unit 9 — Four-Quadrant ΔG Table (memorize)
> | ΔH | ΔS | Spontaneous? |
> |---|---|---|
> | − | + | Always |
> | + | − | Never |
> | − | − | Low T only |
> | + | + | High T only |

## Core Formula Reference

> [!abstract] Quick Equations — All Units

**Unit 1**
$$n = \frac{m}{M} \qquad N = n \times N_A \qquad \bar{m} = \sum(\text{isotope mass} \times \text{fractional abundance})$$

**Unit 3**
$$PV = nRT \qquad M_1V_1 = M_2V_2 \qquad A = \varepsilon lc \qquad E = \frac{hc}{\lambda}$$

**Unit 5**
$$\text{rate} = k[A]^m[B]^n \qquad \ln[A] = -kt + \ln[A]_0 \qquad t_{1/2} = \frac{0.693}{k} \qquad k = Ae^{-E_a/RT}$$

**Unit 6**
$$q = mc\Delta T \qquad \Delta H_{rxn}° = \sum\Delta H_{f,\text{prod}}° - \sum\Delta H_{f,\text{react}}°$$

**Unit 7/8**
$$K_{eq} = \frac{[\text{products}]^{\text{coeff}}}{[\text{reactants}]^{\text{coeff}}} \qquad \text{pH} = -\log[\text{H}_3\text{O}^+] \qquad \text{pH} = \text{p}K_a + \log\frac{[\text{A}^-]}{[\text{HA}]}$$

**Unit 9**
$$\Delta G° = \Delta H° - T\Delta S° \qquad \Delta G° = -nFE° \qquad E = E° - \frac{0.0592}{n}\log Q$$

## Exam Format & Strategy

> [!important] AP Exam Structure
> **Section I — MCQ:** 60 questions, 90 min (no calculator first 25, calculator allowed rest)
> **Section II — FRQ:** 7 questions, 105 min (calculator allowed)
> - 3 long FRQs (10 pts each) + 4 short FRQs (4 pts each)
> - Reference sheet provided (formula table + periodic table)

**See: [[AP Chem FRQ & MCQ Reference Guide]]** for unit-by-unit answer templates and MCQ pattern recognition tables.

> [!tip] FRQ Strategy
> - "Justify" = evidence + reasoning (not just a conclusion)
> - "Explain" = mechanism or why (not just what happens)
> - "Predict" = answer + one-sentence reason
> - Always write units and significant figures
> - If a calculation is wrong, subsequent parts graded on follow-through

> [!tip] MCQ Strategy
> - Cross off answers you can eliminate — even eliminating 1 improves odds
> - Unit 3 and 8 questions dominate — know them cold
> - Anything with "most likely" or "best explains" is testing conceptual reasoning, not calculation

## Self-Assessment Checklist

Use this before each study session to target weak areas.

### Unit 1
- [ ] Can convert between mass, moles, and particles without a formula sheet
- [ ] Can read a PES spectrum and identify core vs. valence electrons
- [ ] Can predict and explain periodic trend differences (IE, EN, radius)

### Unit 2
- [ ] Can draw Lewis structures with correct formal charges
- [ ] Can determine molecular geometry and bond angles from any Lewis structure
- [ ] Know hybridization rules and can assign sp/sp²/sp³ from structure

### Unit 3 ⭐
- [ ] Can rank boiling points and solubility using IMF reasoning
- [ ] Can solve ideal gas law problems with unit conversions
- [ ] Can use $A = \varepsilon lc$ to find concentration from absorbance
- [ ] Know all solid types (metallic/ionic/molecular/network) and their properties
- [ ] Can draw and interpret phase diagrams

### Unit 4
- [ ] Can balance any redox reaction using half-reaction method
- [ ] Can write net ionic equations
- [ ] Can find limiting reagent and calculate theoretical yield

### Unit 5
- [ ] Can determine rate law orders from a data table
- [ ] Can use integrated rate law to find concentration at any time
- [ ] Can identify rate-determining step in a mechanism from rate law

### Unit 6
- [ ] Can apply Hess's Law to multi-step problems
- [ ] Can use bond enthalpies to estimate ΔH
- [ ] Can set up and solve a calorimetry problem ($q_{rxn} = -q_{sol}$)

### Unit 7
- [ ] Can write a Kc expression (remember: omit solids/pure liquids)
- [ ] Can set up and solve an ICE table
- [ ] Can apply Le Chatelier's to any stress (concentration, pressure, temperature)

### Unit 8 ⭐
- [ ] Can calculate pH of weak acid/base using ICE table
- [ ] Can solve all regions of a titration curve
- [ ] Can apply Henderson-Hasselbalch to buffer problems
- [ ] Know the effect of adding strong acid/base to a buffer

### Unit 9
- [ ] Can use the four-quadrant table to predict spontaneity at different temperatures
- [ ] Can calculate ΔG° from ΔH° and ΔS°, and relate to K
- [ ] Can solve Faraday's Law problems (charge → moles of electrons → mass deposited)
- [ ] Know anode/cathode, galvanic vs. electrolytic cell setup
