**Question 1 — Electrode mass change in a galvanic cell**

 **Answer: (A)**

 **Step-by-step reasoning:**Identify which electrode is which using E°E° E° values. Ag⁺/Ag has E°E° E° = +0.80 V (higher) → cathode → reduction occurs here. Ni²⁺/Ni has E°E° E° = −0.25 V (lower) → anode → oxidation occurs here. At the cathode, Ag⁺ ions from solution are reduced and deposit as solid Ag onto the electrode — the cathode **gains mass**. At the anode, solid Ni dissolves into solution as Ni²⁺ — the anode **loses mass**.

 **Why the others are wrong:** (B) Molar mass is irrelevant — mass change is determined by oxidation/reduction, not atomic weight. (C) Ni being more reactive is true, but that means it's oxidized and _loses_ mass, not gains it. (D) Reaction rate has no bearing on which electrode gains mass; also not supported by the data given.

 **The pattern this tests:** Cathode = reduction = metal deposits = mass increases; anode = oxidation = metal dissolves = mass decreases.
**Question 2 — Salt bridge ion flow in a galvanic cell**

 **Answer: (A)**

 **Step-by-step reasoning:**First identify the electrodes from the reaction: Cu(s)+2 Ag+(aq)→2 Ag(s)+Cu 2+(aq)Cu(s) + 2 Ag^+(aq) \rightarrow 2 Ag(s) + Cu^{2+}(aq) Cu(s)+2 Ag+(aq)→2 Ag(s)+Cu 2+(aq). Cu is oxidized → **Cu = anode**. Ag⁺ is reduced → **Ag = cathode**.

 Track charge buildup in each half-cell as the cell runs. At the Cu anode, Cu dissolves as Cu²⁺ → the Cu half-cell **accumulates positive charge** → needs anions (NO₃⁻) to flow in. At the Ag cathode, Ag⁺ ions are consumed as they deposit as solid → the Ag half-cell **loses positive charge** → needs cations (Na⁺) to flow in.

 Therefore Na⁺ flows toward the Ag (cathode) half-cell. (A) has the correct ion and correct direction.

 **Why the others are wrong:** (B) NO₃⁻ toward Ag is the wrong direction — the Ag half-cell needs cations, not anions. (C) Na⁺ toward Cu is the wrong direction — the Cu half-cell needs anions to neutralize building Cu²⁺. (D) NO₃⁻ toward Cu has the correct direction but the stated reason is self-contradicting — electrons flow _away_ from Cu through the wire, not toward it.

 **The pattern this tests:** Cations always migrate toward the cathode; anions always migrate toward the anode — because each half-cell needs to neutralize its own charge buildup.
