## Q1. Common-Ion Effect on Solubility (Ksp / Q)

The particle diagram represents the dissolution of CuCl₂(s) assuming equilibrium at 25°C for Cu²⁺ ions at about 4 × 10⁻⁴ M in a saturated solution. The equilibrium is: CuCl₂(s) ⇌ Cu²⁺(aq) + 2Cl⁻(aq). Which of the following changes to the particle diagram best represents the effect of adding 1.0 mL of 4 M NaCl to the solution?

- A) Some of the Cu²⁺ and Cl⁻ ions combine to form CuCl₂(s) because the Ksp will be lower than 1.6 × 10⁻⁷
- B) Some of the Cu²⁺ and Cl⁻ ions combine to form CuCl₂(s) because the molar solubility will be lower than 4 × 10⁻⁴ M
- C) More Cu²⁺ and Cl⁻ ions will be in solution because the molar solubility will be higher than 4 × 10⁻⁴ M
- D) More Cu²⁺ and Cl⁻ ions will be in solution because the Ksp will be higher than 1.6 × 10⁻⁷

**Answer: B — Precipitation occurs; molar solubility decreases (common-ion effect)**

Adding NaCl introduces Cl⁻ ions, raising [Cl⁻] above the equilibrium value. This causes Q = [Cu²⁺][Cl⁻]² to exceed Ksp, so the reaction shifts left and CuCl₂(s) precipitates — some Cu²⁺ and Cl⁻ ions leave solution. The Ksp itself is a constant at fixed temperature and does not change; only the molar solubility of CuCl₂ decreases. This is the common-ion effect: adding a shared ion forces more precipitation.

> **Trap:** Ksp is temperature-dependent only — it does not change when you add ions. Choice A is wrong because it claims Ksp decreases. Choice D is wrong because Ksp never increases due to adding ions.

## Q2. Particle Diagrams and Entropy (CuCl₂ Dissolution)

Which of the following best explains what the particle diagram is able to show about the entropy change for the dissolution of CuCl₂(s) in pure water?

- A) The particle diagram shows that the dissociation of CuCl₂(s) into ions contributes to an increase in the entropy for the dissolution
- B) The particle diagram shows that the dissociation of CuCl₂(s) into ions contributes to a decrease in the entropy for the dissolution
- C) The particle diagram shows that there is no reorganization of the water molecules around the ions and the change in entropy for the dissolution is zero
- D) The particle diagram shows that there are no interactions between the water molecules and the change in entropy for the dissolution is zero

**Answer: A — Dissociation disperses ions; particle diagram illustrates entropy increase**

When CuCl₂(s) dissolves, the highly ordered crystal lattice breaks apart and Cu²⁺ and Cl⁻ ions become distributed throughout solution — a far more disordered state. A particle diagram that shows ions spread throughout the solution volume visually represents this dispersal, which corresponds to an increase in entropy (ΔS > 0). Particle diagrams correctly communicate that dissolving a solid into ions increases disorder.

> **Trap:** The diagram does NOT show water molecule reorganization (hydration shells) around the ions — that contribution actually decreases entropy and cannot be read from a typical particle diagram. The diagram only shows the dispersal side of the entropy story, not the total ΔS.

## Q4. Nernst Equation — Pacemaker Battery Potential Decrease

Pacemakers are electronic devices that help regulate heart rate. Lithium-iodine cells (operating E = +2.80 V) are commonly used. Zinc-mercury cells (operating E = +1.35 V) were used in earlier pacemakers. After one year of operation, the cell potential of a lithium-iodine cell decreases by 1–2%. Which of the following best explains the cause of the decrease in cell potential?

- A) E_cell = E°_red(cathode) − E°_red(anode), and as the cell operates, E°_red(cathode) decreases
- B) E_cell = E°_red(cathode) − E°_red(anode), and as the cell operates, E°_red(anode) increases
- C) E_cell = E°_cell − (RT/nF)ln Q, and as the cell operates, Q decreases
- D) E_cell = E°_cell − (RT/nF)ln Q, and as the cell operates, Q increases

**Answer: D — Nernst equation; Q increases as reactants are consumed → E_cell decreases**

As the lithium-iodine cell operates, Li and I₂ are consumed and LiI accumulates. This means Q (ratio of products to reactants) continuously increases. By the Nernst equation, E_cell = E°_cell − (RT/nF)ln Q: as ln Q increases, the subtracted term grows larger, and E_cell decreases toward zero. Standard reduction potentials (E°_red) are thermodynamic constants and do not change as the cell operates — they are properties of the half-reactions at standard conditions, not of the operating state.

> **Trap:** E°_red values are constants — they cannot "decrease" or "increase" as the cell operates (eliminates A and B). Q INCREASES (not decreases) as products accumulate, which is what makes E_cell fall (eliminates C).

## Q5. Comparing Zinc-Mercury vs. Zinc-Air Thermodynamic Favorability

The use of zinc-mercury cells in hearing aids has been replaced by zinc-air cells. A zinc-air cell operates using the half-reactions from the zinc-mercury table: ZnO + H₂O + 2e⁻ → Zn + 2OH⁻ (E°red = −1.25 V) as anode and O₂ + 2H₂O + 4e⁻ → 4OH⁻ (E°red = +0.40 V) as cathode. Which of the following provides the most reasonable thermodynamic explanation for why zinc-air cells replaced zinc-mercury cells?

- A) The greater standard cell potential of the zinc-mercury cell compared to the zinc-air cell indicates that the zinc-mercury reaction is more thermodynamically favorable
- B) The greater standard cell potential of the zinc-air cell compared to the zinc-mercury cell indicates that the zinc-air reaction is more thermodynamically favorable
- C) Both reactions are equally thermodynamically favorable because the anode reaction is the same in both cells
- D) The greater standard cell potential of the zinc-air cell indicates greater thermodynamic favorability, and fewer moles of hydroxide are required compared to the zinc-mercury reaction

**Answer: B — Zinc-air cell has higher E°cell; thermodynamically more favorable**

The zinc-air cell uses O₂ as cathode: E°cell = 0.40 − (−1.25) = +1.65 V. The zinc-mercury cell has E°cell = +1.35 V. Since E°cell is directly related to ΔG° (ΔG° = −nFE°cell), a higher E°cell corresponds to a more negative ΔG° and greater thermodynamic favorability. The zinc-air cell's higher cell potential means it releases more free energy per mole of reaction — a thermodynamic advantage over zinc-mercury.

> **Trap:** A higher operating cell potential always corresponds to more thermodynamic favorability (more negative ΔG°). Confusing "replaces" with "less favorable" is a common error — the replacement occurred because zinc-air is BETTER thermodynamically AND because mercury is toxic.

## Q8. Entropy of Ideal Gas Expansion

A sample of an ideal gas at pressure P_i is confined to one chamber of an apparatus. The other chamber is evacuated (0 atm). The valve is opened and the gas expands at constant temperature to fill both chambers (P_f = P_i/2). Which best describes ΔS for this process?

- A) ΔS < 0 because the pressure of the gas decreases by half
- B) ΔS = 0 because there is no change in the total number of particles
- C) ΔS = 0 because the average kinetic energy of the gas particles is unchanged
- D) ΔS > 0 because the gas particles become dispersed in a larger volume

**Answer: D — ΔS > 0; particles dispersed in larger volume = more microstates**

When the gas expands into the evacuated chamber, particles that were confined to a small volume are now distributed across twice the volume. More volume means more possible positions and velocities for each particle — more accessible microstates — so entropy increases. This is a spontaneous irreversible expansion even though temperature is constant and no work is done.

> **Trap:** B and C are wrong because entropy is NOT solely a function of particle count or temperature — it also depends on volume and the number of accessible positions/microstates. A lower pressure does not decrease entropy; more volume at lower pressure = more disorder.

## Q10. Calculating S° for F₂ from ΔS°_rxn

Se(s) + 3F₂(g) → SeF₆(g), ΔS°_rxn = −337 J/(mol_rxn·K). S° values: Se(s) = 42, F₂(g) = ?, SeF₆(g) = 314 J/(mol·K). Which expression correctly calculates S° for F₂(g)?

- A) S° = [−337 + 314 − 42] J/(mol·K)
- B) S° = −[−337 − 314 + 42] J/(mol·K)
- C) S° = (1/3)[−337 + 314 − 42] J/(mol·K)
- D) S° = −(1/3)[−337 − 314 + 42] J/(mol·K)

**Answer: D — Solve algebraically; answer = (1/3)(337 + 314 − 42) ≈ 203 J/(mol·K)**

Set up: ΔS°_rxn = S°(products) − S°(reactants)
−337 = [314] − [42 + 3·S°(F₂)]
−337 = 314 − 42 − 3·S°(F₂)
3·S°(F₂) = 314 − 42 + 337 = 609
S°(F₂) = (1/3)(609) = −(1/3)[−337 − 314 + 42] ✓

This gives S°(F₂) ≈ 203 J/(mol·K), close to the literature value of 202.8 J/(mol·K).

> **Trap:** A forgets to divide by the stoichiometric coefficient of 3 (F₂). B forgets to divide by 3 AND uses wrong algebra. C gets the algebra inside the bracket wrong (−337 + 314 − 42 = −65, giving a negative S°, which is impossible for a gas).

## Q11. Largest ΔS° from Standard Entropy Table

The table provides S° values: C(s) = 6, CO(g) = 198, CO₂(g) = 214, CH₄(g) = 186, C₂H₆(g) = 229, H₂(g) = 131, O₂(g) = 205 J/(mol·K). Which reaction has the largest increase in entropy, ΔS°?

- A) 2C(s) + O₂(g) → 2CO(g)

- C) C(s) + 2H₂(g) → CH₄(g)
- D) 2C(s) + 3H₂(g) → C₂H₆(g)

**Answer: A — ΔS° = +179 J/(mol·K); largest positive value**

Calculate all four:

Reaction A wins because it converts 1 mol gas + 2 mol solid into 2 mol gas — net moles of gas increase from 1 to 2 while 2 solids disappear.

> **Trap:** Don't just look at the number of gas molecules — include both the solid→gas and the stoichiometric coefficients. Reaction D has 3 mol gas → 1 mol gas (net decrease) and is strongly negative.

## Q12. Calculating S°(H₂O) from Glucose Combustion ΔS°

C₆H₁₂O₆(s) + 6O₂(g) → 6CO₂(g) + 6H₂O(g), ΔS°_rxn = +900 J/(mol_rxn·K). S° values: C₆H₁₂O₆(s) = 209, O₂(g) = 205, H₂O(g) = ?, CO₂(g) = 214 J/(mol·K). Which expression calculates S°(H₂O(g))?

## Q13. ΔG° from ΔG°_f Values — Fluorination of Ammonia

NH₃(g) + 3F₂(g) → NF₃(g) + 3HF(g). ΔG°_f values (kJ/mol): NH₃ = −16, F₂ = 0, NF₃ = −91, HF = −280. Which statement best explains whether the reaction is thermodynamically favored at 298 K?

- A) ΔG°_rxn << 0 and favored because product molecules have more complex structures and greater absolute entropies
- B) ΔG°_rxn >> 0 and not favored because total moles of gas-phase products is not greater than reactants
- C) ΔG°_rxn << 0 and favored because energy released forming bonds in products > energy absorbed breaking bonds in reactants
- D) ΔG°_rxn >> 0 and not favored because energy released forming bonds in products < energy absorbed breaking bonds in reactants

**Answer: C — ΔG° = −915 kJ/mol; driven by very stable HF and NF₃ bond formation**

ΔG°_rxn = [−91 + 3(−280)] − [(−16) + 0] = [−91 − 840] − [−16] = −931 + 16 = **−915 kJ/mol ≪ 0**

The reaction is strongly thermodynamically favored. The dominant driving force is enthalpic: forming H−F bonds (ΔG°_f(HF) = −280 kJ/mol each, reflecting very strong bonds) releases far more energy than is absorbed breaking N−H bonds in NH₃ and F−F bonds in F₂. ## Q14. KOH Dissolution — Justifying Thermodynamic Favorability

A 5.00 g sample of KOH(s) is added to 100.0 g H₂O at room temperature. All KOH dissolves and the temperature increases. Which best justifies that dissolution of KOH(s) is thermodynamically favorable?

- A) Ion-dipole forces stronger than water–water forces → ΔH < 0; ions become less dispersed → ΔS > 0; therefore ΔG < 0
- B) Energy to break ion-ion bonds < energy released forming ion-dipole attractions → ΔH < 0; ions become more widely dispersed → ΔS > 0; therefore ΔG < 0
- C) Average kinetic energy increases → ΔH > 0; ions become more widely dispersed → ΔS > 0; therefore ΔG > 0
- D) Average kinetic energy increases → ΔH > 0; ions become more widely dispersed → ΔS < 0; therefore ΔG > 0

**Answer: B — ΔH < 0 (exothermic), ΔS > 0 (dispersal), ΔG < 0 (favored)**

The temperature increase confirms the dissolution is exothermic (ΔH < 0): the energy released forming ion-dipole attractions between K⁺/OH⁻ and water exceeds the lattice energy needed to separate the ions. Simultaneously, the ions disperse throughout solution (solid → aqueous), increasing entropy (ΔS > 0). With both ΔH < 0 and ΔS > 0, ΔG = ΔH − TΔS < 0 at all temperatures — the process is always thermodynamically favorable.

> **Trap:** An increase in temperature indicates exothermic (ΔH < 0), not ΔH > 0 (eliminates C and D). Choice A incorrectly says ions become "less dispersed" when dissolving — they clearly become MORE dispersed.

## Q15. Ice Melting Above 0°C — Gibbs Free Energy Analysis

An ice cube at 0°C melts when placed in a room at 22°C (295 K). Based on enthalpy, entropy, and Gibbs free energy, which best explains why this process is thermodynamically favorable?

- A) ΔH < 0, ΔS < 0; at T > 273 K, TΔS is smaller than ΔH → ΔG < 0
- B) ΔH < 0, ΔS > 0; at T > 273 K, TΔS is greater than ΔH → ΔG > 0
- C) ΔH > 0, ΔS < 0; at T > 273 K, TΔS is smaller than ΔH → ΔG > 0
- D) ΔH > 0, ΔS > 0; at T > 273 K, TΔS is greater than ΔH → ΔG < 0

**Answer: D — ΔH > 0, ΔS > 0; at T > 273 K, entropy term dominates → ΔG < 0**

Melting requires energy input (endothermic, ΔH > 0). Converting ice (solid) to liquid increases disorder (ΔS > 0). At exactly 0°C (273 K), ΔG = 0 (equilibrium between solid and liquid). Above 273 K, the TΔS term grows larger than ΔH, making ΔG = ΔH − TΔS < 0 → the process becomes thermodynamically favorable. At room temperature (295 K > 273 K), melting is spontaneous.

> **Trap:** Melting is ENDOTHERMIC (requires energy), so ΔH > 0 — not exothermic. This is the ΔH > 0, ΔS > 0 quadrant (entropy-driven), where the temperature determines favorability: above T_threshold = ΔH/ΔS, the reaction is favored.

## Q16. Competing Reactions — Activation Energy and Temperature

Substance X undergoes two competing reactions: X ⇌ Y (reaction 1, lower Ea) and X ⇌ Z (reaction 2, higher Ea). At low temperature, Y forms but almost no Z. At high temperature, both form. Which best explains why very little Z forms at low temperature?

- A) A smaller number of X molecules had enough energy to overcome the activation energy barrier for reaction 2, and very little Z was produced
- B) The higher average kinetic energy of X molecules at lower temperature resulted in very little Z produced
- C) The greater collision frequency between X molecules at lower temperature resulted in very little Z
- D) The intermolecular forces between X molecules were much weaker, and very little Z was produced

**Answer: A — Fewer molecules have enough energy to overcome the higher Ea of reaction 2**

Reaction 2 has a higher activation energy barrier than reaction 1. At low temperature, the distribution of kinetic energies among X molecules is narrow — very few molecules possess enough energy to overcome the larger Ea required for reaction 2. Almost all collisions only have enough energy to cross the lower barrier to form Y. At high temperature, the broader energy distribution means more molecules can surmount either barrier, so both Y and Z form.

> **Trap:** B says "higher average kinetic energy at lower temperature" — completely backwards; lower temperature means lower average kinetic energy. C and D (collision frequency, intermolecular forces) are not relevant to explaining selectivity between two competing pathways.

## Q17. Kinetics vs. Thermodynamics — Diamond Synthesis

Graphite is converted into cubic diamond (thermodynamically more stable) through a natural process taking billions of years. Synthetic diamonds, made in about one week, have carbon atoms in a hexagonal lattice — even though cubic diamond is thermodynamically more stable. Which best justifies why the synthetic process produces hexagonal diamond instead of the thermodynamically more stable cubic diamond?

- B) The energy required to create new bonds in cubic diamond is much smaller than in hexagonal
- C) The activation energy to form cubic diamond is much less than to form hexagonal diamond
- D) The activation energy to form cubic diamond is much greater than to form hexagonal diamond

**Answer: D — Cubic diamond has much greater activation energy; kinetics favors hexagonal**

Cubic diamond is thermodynamically more stable (lower Gibbs free energy / more stable product), but it is kinetically inaccessible under laboratory conditions because the activation energy to form the cubic lattice structure is very large. > **Trap:** The most thermodynamically stable product is NOT always the one that forms — kinetics controls which pathway is accessible. This is the classic kinetic vs. thermodynamic control distinction. C is wrong (lower Ea for cubic diamond would make it form faster, not hexagonal).

## Q18. ATP Hydrolysis — Thermodynamics vs. Kinetics

## Q19. Relating K to ΔG° — N₂ + O₂ → 2NO

## Q20. Connecting Equilibrium Position to ΔG°

- B) At equilibrium, for reaction 1: [Product]/[Reactant] >> 1 and ΔG°_rxn > 0; for reaction 2: [Product]/[Reactant] << 1 and ΔG°_rxn < 0

## Q21. Threshold Temperature — Tungsten Melting

- C) Will melt because T > ΔH°_fus / ΔS°_fus

**Answer: C — T (3723 K) > ΔH/ΔS (3500 K) → ΔG < 0 → melting is favorable**

## Q22. Coupled Reactions — Can a Particle Diagram Represent Energy Changes?

Reaction 1: FeO(s) + CO(g) → Fe(l) + CO₂(g), ΔG°_rxn > 0

**Answer: C — Particle diagrams cannot represent the energy accounting that drives coupling favorability**

The reason the overall reaction is thermodynamically favorable is that the large negative ΔG° of reaction 2 more than offsets the positive ΔG° of reaction 1. This is fundamentally an energy argument — ΔG° values are summed algebraically. Particle diagrams show particle counts, positions, and phases; they CANNOT show ΔG° values, energy changes, or which energy contribution dominates. The coupling works through energy balance, not visible particle-level phenomena.

> **Trap:** A (volume of gas) might seem relevant, but volume increase relates to entropy not to the full ΔG° picture. ## Q23. Electrolysis of NaCl — Thermodynamic Favorability

NaCl decomposition (electrolysis) requires an external power source — this is the defining feature of an electrolytic cell, which drives non-spontaneous reactions (ΔG > 0). If the reaction were thermodynamically favorable (ΔG < 0), no voltage source would be needed.

## Q24. Electrolysis of Water — Identifying a Flaw in the Diagram

## Q25. Galvanic Cell Modifications — Greatest Increase in E°_cell

- A) Replace Cu half-cell with Ag-AgNO₃; E°cell = +0.67 V (1 mol e⁻ reduces 1 mol Ag⁺)
- B) Replace Cu half-cell with Ag-AgNO₃; E°cell = +0.93 V
- C) Replace Pb half-cell with Al-Al(NO₃)₃; oxidation of Al more favorable than Pb; E°cell = +2.00 V
- D) Replace Pb half-cell with Al-Al(NO₃)₃; 6 mol e⁻ flow; E°cell = +5.66 V

**Answer: C — Al anode gives E°cell = 0.34 − (−1.66) = +2.00 V; greatest increase from 0.47 V**

Replacing the Pb anode with Al:
E°cell = E°red(Cu cathode) − E°red(Al anode) = 0.34 − (−1.66) = **+2.00 V**
Increase = 2.00 − 0.47 = +1.53 V

Option B (Ag cathode) gives E°cell = 0.80 − (−0.13) = +0.93 V (increase of +0.46 V) — less than C.

Choice C's reasoning is also correct: Al has a more negative E°red, meaning Al is more easily oxidized (stronger reducing agent), making the oxidation more thermodynamically favorable. This drives a larger cell potential.

> **Trap:** D claims E°cell = +5.66 V — this happens by multiplying E° by the number of electrons (6). E° is an INTENSIVE property; it does not multiply with stoichiometric coefficients. A gets the math wrong (0.80 − 0.13 = 0.67, but should be 0.80 − (−0.13) = 0.93).

## Q27. Fuel Cell — Calculating E°_red(cathode) from ΔG°

2H₂(g) + O₂(g) → 2H₂O(l), ΔG°_rxn = −474 kJ/mol_rxn. Cathode: O₂(g) + 4H⁺(aq) + 4e⁻ → 2H₂O(l), E° = ?. Anode: 2H⁺(aq) + 2e⁻ → H₂(g), E° = 0.00 V. Which calculates E°_red(cathode)?

- A) E°_red(cathode) = −474 / (4 × 96,500)
- B) E°_red(cathode) = −(−474,000 / (4 × 96,500))
- C) E°_red(cathode) = (4 × 96,500) / 474
- D) E°_red(cathode) = −((4 × 96,500) / 474,000)

**Answer: B — E° = −ΔG°/(nF); convert kJ to J; n = 4; cathode E° = anode 0.00 + E°cell**

E°_cell = E°_red(cathode) − 0.00 → E°_red(cathode) = E°_cell = −(−474,000/(4 × 96,500)) ✓

## Q28. Identifying Cell Type from ΔG° — Ag/Cu Reaction

2Ag(s) + Cu²⁺(aq) → 2Ag⁺(aq) + Cu(s). Standard reduction potentials: Ag⁺/Ag = +0.80 V, Cu²⁺/Cu = +0.34 V. Which identifies the type of electrochemical cell based on ΔG?

- A) Galvanic cell; ΔG = −(96,500 × (0.46)) / 1,000 = −44 kJ/mol
- B) Galvanic cell; ΔG = −(2 × 96,500 × (0.46)) / 1,000 = −89 kJ/mol
- C) Electrolytic cell; ΔG = −(96,500 × (−0.46)) / 1,000 = +44 kJ/mol
- D) Electrolytic cell; ΔG = −(2 × 96,500 × (−0.46)) / 1,000 = +89 kJ/mol

**Answer: D — E°cell = −0.46 V → ΔG = +89 kJ/mol → electrolytic cell**

For this reaction, Ag is oxidized (anode) and Cu²⁺ is reduced (cathode):
E°cell = E°red(cathode: Cu²⁺/Cu) − E°red(anode: Ag⁺/Ag) = 0.34 − 0.80 = **−0.46 V**

Since n = 2 (two electrons from 2Ag → 2Ag⁺):
ΔG = −nFE° = −(2)(96,500)(−0.46) / 1,000 = **+89 kJ/mol > 0** → non-spontaneous → electrolytic cell

## Q29. Nernst Equation — Zn-Ag Cell with KOH Salt Bridge

A Zn-Ag galvanic cell (Zn/Zn(NO₃)₂ and AgNO₃ half-cells) has standard E° = +1.56 V. The KNO₃ salt bridge is replaced with KOH. Zn(OH)₂ precipitates in the Zn half-cell. How is the cell potential affected as Zn(OH)₂ precipitates?

- A) Cell potential increases because [Zn²⁺] decreases and Q = [Zn²⁺]/[Ag⁺]² becomes smaller
- B) Cell potential decreases because [Zn²⁺] decreases and Q = [Zn²⁺]/[Ag⁺]² becomes smaller
- C) Cell potential increases because K⁺ ions replace Zn²⁺ ions and K⁺ reduction is more favorable
- D) Cell potential stays the same because Zn(OH)₂ is not part of the redox reaction

**Answer: A — [Zn²⁺] decreases → Q decreases → E_cell increases (Nernst)**

Reaction: Zn(s) + 2Ag⁺(aq) → Zn²⁺(aq) + 2Ag(s), Q = [Zn²⁺]/[Ag⁺]²

When Zn(OH)₂ precipitates, [Zn²⁺] decreases → Q decreases → ln Q decreases (more negative). By Nernst: E_cell = E° − (RT/nF)ln Q → subtracting a more negative number = adding a positive term → **E_cell increases** above E°.

This is analogous to Le Chatelier's principle: removing a product (Zn²⁺ precipitates out) drives the forward reaction, which means the cell is MORE spontaneous than standard conditions.

> **Trap:** B gets the direction backwards — when Q decreases for a spontaneous reaction, E_cell INCREASES (not decreases). The Nernst equation subtracts (RT/nF)ln Q; smaller ln Q makes E_cell larger, not smaller.

## Q30. Nernst Equation — Ni-Cd Cell with [Ni²⁺] > 1 M

- A) Ratio [Cd²⁺]/[Ni²⁺] is smaller if [Ni²⁺] > 1 M → Q < 1 → E_cell > E°_cell

- D) Ratio [Ni²⁺]/[Cd²⁺] is larger if [Ni²⁺] > 1 M → Q < 1 → E_cell < E°_cell

**Answer: A — Q = [Cd²⁺]/[Ni²⁺] < 1 when [Ni²⁺] > 1 M → E_cell > E°_cell**

Q = [Cd²⁺]/[Ni²⁺] (product over reactant; solids excluded). If [Ni²⁺] > 1 M and [Cd²⁺] = 1 M: Q = 1/(>1) < 1. By Nernst: E = E° − (RT/nF)ln Q. ## Q31. Faraday's Law — Why Ag Gains More Mass than Cd

Two spoons are electroplated: Cell 1 with Ag⁺ (n = 1), Cell 2 with Cd²⁺ (n = 2). Current = 5.00 A for 600 s. The Ag spoon gains much more mass than the Cd spoon. Which equation best accounts for this difference?

- A) ΔG° = ΔH° − TΔS°
- B) ΔG° = −RT ln K
- C) E = E° − (RT/nF) ln Q
- D) I = q/t

**Answer: D — I = q/t; same charge but Ag requires only 1 e⁻ per atom vs Cd's 2 e⁻**

From I = q/t → q = It = (5.00)(600) = 3,000 C of charge flows in both cells.

- Moles Ag: n_e = 3,000/96,500; n_Ag = n_e/1 (1 e⁻ per Ag⁺) → more mol Ag
- Moles Cd: n_e = 3,000/96,500; n_Cd = n_e/2 (2 e⁻ per Cd²⁺) → half the mol

Same charge → same moles of electrons → twice as many Ag atoms deposited as Cd atoms. With Ag (107.87 g/mol) and Cd (112.41 g/mol) having similar molar masses, the dominant factor is n: Ag needs only 1 electron per atom while Cd needs 2. Hence Ag deposit has ~2× more moles and ~2× more mass.

> **Trap:** A, B, C are thermodynamics/electrochemistry equations unrelated to calculating mass from current. Only D (I = q/t, from which q = It) directly enables the Faraday calculation.

## Q32. Faraday's Law — Identifying Highest Current Required

Four identical objects are electroplated: Objects 1 & 2 with Ag (Ag⁺ + e⁻ → Ag), Objects 3 & 4 with Zn (Zn²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Zn). Each deposits 0.10 mol metal. Object 1 & 3 take 30 min; Objects 2 & 4 take 60 min. Which requires the highest current?

- A) Object 1, based on molar mass of Ag and time of operation
- B) Object 2, based on molar mass of Ag and time of operation
- C) Object 3, based on coulombs of charge needed and time of operation
- D) Object 4, based on coulombs of charge needed and time of operation

**Answer: C — Object 3 (Zn, 30 min): needs 2× charge (n=2) in shortest time**

Calculate required charge for each metal:
- Ag (n = 1): q = 0.10 mol × 1 × 96,500 = 9,650 C
- Zn (n = 2): q = 0.10 mol × 2 × 96,500 = 19,300 C

Then I = q/t:
- Object 1 (Ag, 1800 s): I = 9,650/1800 = **5.36 A**
- Object 2 (Ag, 3600 s): I = 9,650/3600 = **2.68 A**
- Object 3 (Zn, 1800 s): I = 19,300/1800 = **10.72 A** ← **highest**
- Object 4 (Zn, 3600 s): I = 19,300/3600 = **5.36 A**

Object 3 requires the highest current because it combines the larger charge requirement (2 electrons per Zn) with the shortest time.

> **Trap:** Molar mass is NOT the determining factor — Objects 1 and 4 have the same current despite different metals, because the charge requirement and time exactly compensate. The key variables are n (electrons per ion) and t (time available).

## Q33. Faraday's Law — Au vs. Ag Electroplating

Two cells plate the same number of moles of Au or Ag in the same time. Au³⁺ + 3e⁻ → Au (E° = +1.50 V); Ag⁺ + e⁻ → Ag (E° = +0.80 V). Which relationship determines how much more current is needed to electroplate with Au than Ag?

- A) The difference in standard reduction potential of Au³⁺ and Ag⁺ is 0.70 V
- B) Three times more electrons are needed to reduce Au³⁺(aq) to Au(s) than to reduce Ag⁺(aq) to Ag(s)

For the same moles of metal deposited in the same time:
