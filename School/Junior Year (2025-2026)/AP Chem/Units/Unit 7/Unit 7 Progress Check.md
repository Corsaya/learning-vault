## Q1. Acetone in sealed container — pressure data table

A sample of acetone is placed into a container. The container is sealed and attached to a pressure sensor, as shown in the diagram above. The container is allowed to sit on the lab table for a few minutes as the pressure in the container is monitored at regular intervals. At the end of 2.00 minutes, some acetone liquid remains in the container. Which of the following best explains the pressure data presented in the table above?

**Answer: C — The acetone vaporizes from the liquid at a constant rate, the rate of condensation increases until it becomes equal to the rate of evaporation, and then the pressure stays constant.**

Initially, acetone molecules escape the liquid phase faster than they return. As vapor accumulates, the condensation rate increases until it equals the evaporation rate — dynamic equilibrium. At that point, pressure stops rising and holds constant. Because liquid remains at 2.00 min, the system hasn't run out of liquid; it has reached vapor–liquid equilibrium.

> **Trap:** Choosing B (completely vaporized). If all liquid had evaporated, adding more liquid would not restore the plateau — but it does. A pressure plateau with liquid still present always signals vapor–liquid equilibrium, not complete evaporation. Choosing D (vaporization stops) misidentifies equilibrium as cessation of a process; both evaporation and condensation continue at equal rates.

The reaction converts 2 mol gas → 1 mol gas, so total pressure decreases as equilibrium is approached. When pressure stabilizes, concentrations are no longer changing — equilibrium is established. ## Q7. $2\ \text{HI}(g) \rightleftharpoons \text{H}_2(g) + \text{I}_2(g)$ — find $K_{eq}$

|---|---|---|

| $\text{H}_2$ | 0.00 | 0.030 |
| $\text{I}_2$ | 0.00 | ? |

In an experiment involving the reaction shown above, a sample of pure $\text{HI}$ was placed inside a rigid container at a certain temperature. The table above provides the initial and equilibrium concentrations for some of the substances in the reaction. Based on the data, which of the following is the value of the equilibrium constant ($K_{eq}$) for the reaction, and why?

**Answer: C — $K_{eq} = 1.6 \times 10^{-2}$, because $[\text{I}_2]_{\text{eq}} = [\text{H}_2]_{\text{eq}}$**

## Q8. $\text{SO}_2(g) + \text{Cl}_2(g) \rightleftharpoons \text{SO}_2\text{Cl}_2(g)$ — find $P_{\text{Cl}_2}$ and $K_p$

$\text{SO}_2(g)$ reacts with $\text{Cl}_2(g)$ to produce $\text{SO}_2\text{Cl}_2(g)$, according to the equation above. A mixture of 1.00 atm of $\text{SO}_2(g)$ and 1.00 atm of $\text{Cl}_2(g)$ is placed in a rigid, evacuated 1.00 L container, and the reaction mixture is allowed to reach equilibrium at a certain temperature. Which of the following indicates the equilibrium value of $P_{\text{Cl}_2}$ and the approximate value of the equilibrium constant, $K_p$?

*(Initial: $P_{\text{SO}_2} = 1.00$, $P_{\text{Cl}_2} = 1.00$, $P_{\text{SO}_2\text{Cl}_2} = 0.00$; Equilibrium: $P_{\text{SO}_2} = 0.20$, $P_{\text{Cl}_2} = ?$, $P_{\text{SO}_2\text{Cl}_2} = 0.80$)*

**Answer: C — $P_{\text{Cl}_2} = 0.20\ \text{atm}$ and $K_p = 20.$**

$P_{\text{SO}_2\text{Cl}_2}$ at equilibrium $= 0.80\ \text{atm}$. By stoichiometry (1:1:1), each reactant decreased by $0.80\ \text{atm}$: $P_{\text{Cl}_2} = 1.00 - 0.80 = 0.20\ \text{atm}$. $K_p = \frac{P_{\text{SO}_2\text{Cl}_2}}{P_{\text{SO}_2} \cdot P_{\text{Cl}_2}} = \frac{0.80}{(0.20)(0.20)} = 20$.

> **Trap:** Using initial pressures instead of equilibrium pressures in $K_p$. Always use ICE-derived equilibrium values. Also watch the 1:1:1 stoichiometry — each mol of product formed consumes exactly 1 mol each of $\text{SO}_2$ and $\text{Cl}_2$.

## Q9. Cis/trans-2-butene equilibrium — $K_c = 1.2$, second vessel has twice the [cis]

The concentration of *cis*-2-butene in the second vessel is twice the concentration in the first vessel. What is the concentration of *trans*-2-butene in the second vessel compared to that in the first vessel?

**Answer: C — Twice the concentration of that in the first vessel**

$K_c = \frac{[\text{trans}]}{[\text{cis}]} = 1.2$ is fixed at constant temperature. Doubling $[\text{cis}]$ requires doubling $[\text{trans}]$ to maintain the same ratio of 1.2. The ratio is what's constant, not the individual concentrations.

> **Trap:** Choosing B (same concentration). Constant $K$ does not mean constant individual concentrations — it means their ratio is constant. Adding reactant shifts the equilibrium until the ratio is restored.

## Q11. $2\ \text{SO}_2(g) + \text{O}_2(g) \rightleftharpoons 2\ \text{SO}_3(g)$, $K_p \approx 2 \times 10^5$, initial $P = 1\ \text{atm}$ each

## Q13. Combining equilibrium constants — find $K_3$

| Reaction | Chemical Equation | $K_{eq}$ |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | $\text{HA}(aq) \rightleftharpoons \text{H}^+(aq) + \text{A}^-(aq)$ | $K_1 = 0.010$ |
| 2 | $\text{H}_2\text{B}(aq) \rightleftharpoons \text{H}^+(aq) + \text{HB}^-(aq)$ | $K_2 = 0.000010$ |

The table above provides the $K_{eq}$ values for two reactions. Which of the following is the correct mathematical expression needed to determine the equilibrium constant of the reaction shown below?

Reaction 3: $\text{HA}(aq) + \text{HB}^-(aq) \rightleftharpoons \text{H}_2\text{B}(aq) + \text{A}^-(aq)$ $\quad K_{eq} = K_3$

**Answer: A — $K_3 = K_1 \times \frac{1}{K_2}$**

Reaction 3 = Reaction 1 (forward) + Reverse of Reaction 2. Reversing Reaction 2 gives $K = 1/K_2$. Adding reactions multiplies their $K$ values: $K_3 = K_1 \times (1/K_2)$.

> **Trap:** Choosing C or D (subtracting $K$ values). Equilibrium constants multiply when reactions are added — they are never added or subtracted. Subtract reactions means dividing $K$ values (multiplying by the reciprocal).

## Q15. $\text{PbCl}_2(s) + 2\ \text{Ag}^+(aq) \rightleftharpoons \text{Pb}^{2+}(aq) + 2\ \text{AgCl}(s)$

Reaction 1: $\text{PbCl}_2(s) \rightleftharpoons \text{Pb}^{2+}(aq) + 2\ \text{Cl}^-(aq) \quad K_{sp} = K_1$

Reaction 2: $\text{AgCl}(s) \rightleftharpoons \text{Ag}^+(aq) + \text{Cl}^-(aq) \quad K_{sp} = K_2$

Based on the information given above, which of the following is the expression for $K_{eq}$ for the reaction that occurs when a $0.1\ M\ \text{AgNO}_3(aq)$ is added to a saturated solution of $\text{PbCl}_2(aq)$?

**Answer: D — $K_{eq} = \dfrac{K_1}{(K_2)^2}$**

The overall reaction = Reaction 1 (forward) + 2 × Reverse(Reaction 2). Reversing Reaction 2 gives $K = 1/K_2$; using it twice gives $(1/K_2)^2$. Multiplying: $K_{eq} = K_1 \times (1/K_2)^2 = K_1/(K_2)^2$.

> **Trap:** Choosing C ($K_1 \times (K_2)^2$). The coefficient of 2 on AgCl means Reaction 2 is reversed and used twice — so its $K$ is inverted and squared: $(1/K_2)^2$, not $(K_2)^2$. Adding $K$ values (choices A and B) is never valid.

## Q19. $\text{X}_2(g) + \text{Y}_2(g) \rightleftharpoons 2\ \text{XY}(g)$, $K_c = 25$ — is system at equilibrium?

| Substance | Concentration ($M$) |
|---|---|
| $\text{X}_2$ | 0.10 |
| $\text{Y}_2$ | 0.30 |
| $\text{XY}$ | 0.20 |

The chemical reaction shown above took place inside a rigid container at constant temperature. The table provides the concentrations of reactants and products at some point during the reaction. Based on this information, which of the following explains whether or not the reaction has reached equilibrium, and why?

**Answer: C — The reaction is not at equilibrium because $Q_c < K_c$; the forward reaction is favored in order to form more $\text{XY}$.**

$Q_c = \frac{[\text{XY}]^2}{[\text{X}_2][\text{Y}_2]} = \frac{(0.20)^2}{(0.10)(0.30)} = \frac{0.040}{0.030} \approx 1.3$. Since $Q_c = 1.3 < K_c = 25$, the system must produce more products (forward reaction favored).

> **Trap:** Choosing B ($Q < K$ → reverse favored). This is backwards. $Q < K$ always means the numerator (products) must increase → forward reaction favored. $Q > K$ → reverse favored.

## Q23. $\text{Hg}_2\text{I}_2(s) \rightleftharpoons \text{Hg}_2^{2+}(aq) + 2\ \text{I}^-(aq)$, $[\text{I}^-] = 4.6 \times 10^{-10}\ M$

## Q24. $\text{AgI}(s) \rightleftharpoons \text{Ag}^+(aq) + \text{I}^-(aq)$, $K_{sp} = 8.3 \times 10^{-17}$ — find $S$

The dissolution of $\text{AgI}$ is represented above. Which of the following shows the mathematical relationship between the molar solubility, $S$, and the $K_{sp}$ of $\text{AgI}$ at 298 K?

**Answer: C — $S = \sqrt{8.3 \times 10^{-17}}\ \text{mol/L}$**

AgI is a 1:1 salt: $[\text{Ag}^+] = [\text{I}^-] = S$. $K_{sp} = S \cdot S = S^2$, so $S = \sqrt{K_{sp}} = \sqrt{8.3 \times 10^{-17}}$.

> **Trap:** Choosing A ($S = K_{sp}$). This confuses $K_{sp}$ with solubility directly. For a 1:1 salt, you must take the square root of $K_{sp}$ to get $S$.

## Q25. $\text{Ca(OH)}_2$ in $0.0010\ M\ \text{NaOH}$ vs. distilled water

$\text{Ca(OH)}_2(s) \rightleftharpoons \text{Ca}^{2+}(aq) + 2\ \text{OH}^-(aq) \quad K_{sp} = 5.5 \times 10^{-6}$ at 298 K

The equilibrium in a saturated solution of $\text{Ca(OH)}_2$ is represented above. In an experiment, a student places 5.0 g of $\text{Ca(OH)}_2(s)$ into 100.0 mL of distilled water and stirs the mixture. How would the results be affected if the student repeats the experiment but this time places 5.0 g of $\text{Ca(OH)}_2(s)$ into 100.0 mL of 0.0010 $M$ $\text{NaOH}(aq)$ instead of distilled water?

**Answer: B — Less solid will dissolve, because the larger value of $[\text{OH}^-]$ will cause the equilibrium position to lie farther to the left.**

NaOH provides $[\text{OH}^-]$ before any $\text{Ca(OH)}_2$ dissolves. This common-ion effect suppresses dissolution (Le Chatelier: added product shifts equilibrium left). Less $\text{Ca(OH)}_2$ dissolves.

> **Trap:** Choosing A (larger $[\text{OH}^-]$ → right shift). A right shift would mean more $\text{Ca(OH)}_2$ dissolving, which would increase $[\text{OH}^-]$ further — that's a positive feedback loop that violates Le Chatelier. The added $\text{OH}^-$ is a product stress; the system responds by shifting left.

## Q26. $\text{AgCN}(s) \rightleftharpoons \text{Ag}^+(aq) + \text{CN}^-(aq)$ — add solid NaCN

The dissolution of solid $\text{AgCN}$ is represented by the chemical equation above. In pure water, the equilibrium concentration of $\text{Ag}^+$ ions in a saturated solution is $7.7 \times 10^{-9}\ M$. If a small amount of solid NaCN is added to the saturated $\text{AgCN}$ solution, which of the following would be observed?

**Answer: C — The molar solubility of $\text{AgCN}$ becomes smaller than $7.7 \times 10^{-9}\ M$ and some $\text{AgCN}$ precipitates.**

NaCN dissolves to give $\text{CN}^-$, a common ion (product of AgCN dissolution). Adding a product shifts equilibrium left → more AgCN precipitates → lower molar solubility. $K_{sp}$ itself does not change (temperature is constant).

> **Trap:** Choosing A or B ($K_{sp}$ increases). $K_{sp}$ is a constant at fixed temperature — adding a common ion cannot change $K_{sp}$. Only temperature changes $K_{sp}$.

## Summary of Key Patterns — Unit 7 MCQ

| Pattern | Rule | Questions |
|---|---|---|
| Equilibrium identification | Equilibrium = constant concentrations/pressure; dynamic, not static | Q1, Q2, Q3 |
| Dynamic vs. static equilibrium | Both rates equal and nonzero; single snapshot cannot confirm equilibrium | Q4, Q5 |
| Rate comparison from particle diagrams | Net product increase → forward rate > reverse rate | Q6 |
| ICE table — stoichiometry | Change rows use coefficients; $[\text{I}_2]_{\text{eq}} = [\text{H}_2]_{\text{eq}}$ for equimolar production | Q7, Q8 |
| Large $K$ → near completion | $K \gg 1$: essentially all limiting reactant consumed | Q11, Q12 |
| Small $K$ → reactants favored | $K \ll 1$: products scarce at equilibrium | Q10 |
| Combining equilibria | Reverse → $1/K$; add reactions → multiply $K$ values; multiply reaction → $K^n$ | Q13, Q14, Q15 |
| $Q$ vs. $K$ — direction | $Q < K$ → forward; $Q > K$ → reverse; always write correct $Q$ expression first | Q19, Q20, Q21 |
| Le Chatelier: concentration | Add reactant → forward; add product → reverse | Q16, Q21 |
| Le Chatelier: pressure/volume | Decrease $V$ → shift toward fewer gas moles | Q17 |
| Le Chatelier: temperature | Endothermic: heat is reactant; cool → shift left | Q18 |
| $K_{sp}$ expression | Write dissolution; raise $[\text{ions}]$ to stoichiometric coefficients | Q22 |
| $K_{sp}$ → solubility | 1:1 salt: $S = \sqrt{K_{sp}}$; 2:1 salt: $S = (K_{sp}/4)^{1/3}$ | Q23, Q24 |
| Common-ion effect | Pre-existing product ion suppresses dissolution; $K_{sp}$ unchanged | Q25, Q26 |
