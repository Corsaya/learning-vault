## What this actually means

**Two genuinely stable weak spots** (mid-band on both sittings, not
noise): **Standard English Conventions** and **Advanced Math**. These
are the highest-confidence targets for real content review — they
didn't move when everything else swung, which usually means an actual
knowledge/skill gap rather than a bad day. Advanced Math carries **35%
weight** (13-15 questions) — the single highest-leverage domain to fix.
Problem-Solving & Data Analysis is a secondary target: never top band,
and got worse, but lower weight (15%).

**Four highly volatile domains** (swung 2-3+ performance bands between
sittings): Information and Ideas, Craft and Structure, Expression of
Ideas, Algebra, and especially Geometry and Trigonometry (470-540 →
680-800 is enormous). Big swings on the *same content area* six months
apart usually mean the underlying skill is there but execution
isn't consistent — timing/pacing, fatigue in a specific section
position, careless errors under pressure, or possibly guessing
variance on a small question count (Geometry/Trig is only 5-7
questions, so a few flipped answers moves the band a lot). Content
re-teaching is lower-value here than timed practice volume and
error-pattern review (i.e., redoing missed questions to find *why*,
not just re-learning the concept from scratch).

**Algebra** (this is Unit 1's domain) sits in between: not a stable
weak spot like Advanced Math/Conventions, but it did drop a full band
between sittings (680-800 → 610-670) at the highest question weight
(35%, 13-15Q) alongside Advanced Math. Worth a real review pass, but
the priority ordering below still ranks Advanced Math and Conventions
above it since those never touched the top band at all.

## Recommended priority order for the ~20-day window

1. **Advanced Math** (35% weight, stable mid-band) — highest
   leverage, real content gaps likely (nonlinear functions, quadratics,
   exponentials — the actual digital-SAT "Advanced Math" domain).
2. **Standard English Conventions** (26% weight, stable mid-band) —
   grammar/usage rules are finite and drillable; efficient point gains
   for the time invested.
3. **Algebra** (35% weight, volatile but dropped) — Unit 1 already
   covers this; review + timed practice, not from-scratch reteaching.
4. **Problem-Solving & Data Analysis** (15% weight, stable-to-worse) —
   secondary, lower question count than #1-3.
5. **Geometry/Trig + all Reading/Writing volatile domains** — lowest
   priority for new *content*; treat as timed-practice/consistency
   work layered in throughout, not dedicated study blocks, since the
   swings suggest execution rather than knowledge gaps.
