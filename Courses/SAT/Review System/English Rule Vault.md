## Dashes

**Matched-pair rule** — *from [[Bluebook Practice Test — 2026-08-20]], R&W M1 Q19 (picked a
colon, answer was a dash).*

An interrupter must be closed by the **same mark that opened it**: dash…dash or comma…comma.
Never dash…comma, never dash…colon.

The opening mark is almost always **outside the underlined portion**, so the answer choices
only ever show you one end. **Look left, into the non-underlined text, before choosing.**

- ✅ *…findings indicate that—assuming solar panel installers' cooperation—this practice has…*
- ❌ *…indicate that—assuming solar panel installers' cooperation: this practice has…*

## Verb Tense

**Finite verb vs. participle — does this sentence already have a main verb?** *From
[[Bluebook Practice Test — 2026-08-20]], R&W M2 Q20 and Q21 — the same trap twice in one
module.*

If the sentence already has a subject and a main verb, a second **finite** verb in the blank
creates a comma splice. The blank needs an **-ing / -ed participle** instead.

**Procedure:** find the main verb first. If one already exists and there's no FANBOYS or
subordinator in sight, cross out every finite-verb choice (*had prioritized*, *were searching*,
*has been*) and keep the participle.

- ✅ *…ancient Egyptians slept on hard headrests, their design **prioritizing** airflow…*
- ❌ *…their design **had prioritized** airflow…* (splice — "slept" is already the main verb)
- ✅ *…scientists, **searching** for evidence…, adapted the test…* (main verb is *adapted*)
- ❌ *…scientists **were searching** for evidence…, adapted the test…*

> The tell: the answer choices are all forms of the same verb. That's the SAT asking
> "finite or participle?", not "which tense?"

## Transitions

*Two misses in [[Bluebook Practice Test — 2026-08-20]] (M1 Q23, M2 Q23) — both picked a
transition that fit the topic rather than the logical relationship.*

Cover the choices. Read the sentence before and the sentence after. **Say the relationship out
loud in your own words** ("this contradicts that" / "this is an example of that"). Only then
look at the choices. Plugging each option in and listening for what sounds right is what
produces these misses.
