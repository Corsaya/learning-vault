## The method in one picture

```
        ┌─────────────────────────────────────────────┐
        │   DAILY SRS  (vocab + kanji recognition)     │  ← the backbone, never skip
        │   ~20 min/day · Anki/alternative             │
        └─────────────────────────────────────────────┘
                          +
        ┌──────────────────┐   ┌──────────────────────┐
        │  GRAMMAR (bites) │   │  INPUT (read+listen) │
        │  Tae Kim/Genki/  │   │  graded → native     │
        │  Bunpro          │   │  slightly above level│
        └──────────────────┘   └──────────────────────┘
                          +
        ┌─────────────────────────────────────────────┐
        │  OUTPUT (optional early): handwriting, then  │
        │  speaking/writing later. Low JLPT leverage.  │
        └─────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

The JLPT itself tests only **reading + listening** (no speaking, no handwriting).
That's why this plan front-loads input and SRS. Handwriting and speaking are tracked
as *skills you want*, not as test requirements — see each stage file.

## Your priority order (and what it means)

You ranked: **Reading > Listening > Writing(by hand) > Formal reading > Speaking.**

- **Reading #1** → kanji recognition + vocab SRS + graded reading get the most weekly time. Perfect, because this is also the highest-leverage path for JLPT.
- **Listening #2** → active immersion (anime/media with intent), NHK, podcasts. Your NotebookLM idea lives here — feed it grammar notes to generate audio reviews for passive listening.
- **Writing by hand #3** → a parallel "skill track." Genuinely satisfying, but slow and *not* on the JLPT. Recommendation inside each stage: recognition-first, handwriting in small doses.
- **Formal reading #4** → emerges naturally at N3+ (NHK regular, then news/literature).
- **Speaking #5** → lowest priority by your call. Plan keeps it minimal until N3, then optional (italki/HelloTalk) when/if you want it.

## Golden rules (from people who got fluent)

1. **Consistency beats intensity.** 30 min daily >> 3.5 hrs once a week. Protect the daily SRS streak above all.
2. **Don't over-polish the basics.** Finish kana fast, don't grind Genki forever — get to real input as early as you can tolerate.
3. **Recognition before production.** Learn to *read* kanji before you stress about *writing* them. Saves hundreds of hours.
4. **Immersion early and often.** Start listening to real Japanese (even when you understand ~5%) much sooner than feels reasonable. Your ear needs the runway.
5. **Mine what you actually consume.** Once you can, make flashcards from the anime/manga/games *you* are into. Relevance = retention.
6. **The dip is real.** The intermediate plateau (N3→N2) is where most quit. The plan front-loads habits so you coast through it.
