# Genki vs. Tae Kim — Which Grammar Spine?

Your N5–N4 grammar foundation comes from one of these two. Both are written into
[[Roadmap/Stage 1 - Beginner (N5)|Stage 1]] and [[Roadmap/Stage 2 - Upper Beginner (N4)|Stage 2]].
Pick one as your **spine**; the other can be a free second reference.

## At a glance

| | **Genki I & II** | **Tae Kim's Guide** |
|---|---|---|
| **Cost** | Paid (~$50/book, used/library exist) | **100% free**, online + app |
| **Format** | Structured textbook + workbook | Web guide / app, reference-style |
| **Built for** | Classrooms (the US college standard) | Self-motivated independent learners |
| **Practice** | Lots — exercises, workbook, audio | Minimal — you find your own |
| **Teaching order** | Polite form (です/ます) first | Plain/casual form first |
| **Hand-holding** | High — clear, paced, beginner-safe | Lower — concise, assumes you'll dig |
| **Grammar depth** | Solid, practical | Excellent, often deeper explanations |
| **Pace to N4** | ~23 chapters, very structured | Faster but less scaffolded |

## The real difference

- **Genki gives you a *path*.** Chapters, exercises, a workbook, audio, review sections. You always know what's next and you get practice baked in. Downside: it's designed for a *class* with a teacher, costs money, and can feel slow if you're motivated.
- **Tae Kim gives you the *clearest explanations*, free.** Many learners say his grammar write-ups are the best anywhere. Downside: almost no practice material, and it expects you to be proactive — read it, then go find sentences to apply it. It also teaches casual form first, which feels "backwards" vs. how textbooks/apps usually start (but arguably matches how Japanese is actually spoken).

## Which fits *you*

Your profile: self-study, reading-first, media-driven, free-until-N4, willing to be
proactive (you're literally building a multi-year system in Obsidian).

> [!tip] Recommendation: **Tae Kim as spine + Bunpro (or Anki) for practice**
> - It's **free** (matches your N4 line), explanations are top-tier, and you're clearly the proactive type it's written for.
> - Pair it with **Bunpro** (free trial / cheap) or your own **Anki grammar cards** to supply the practice Tae Kim lacks. That combo neutralizes its one real weakness.
> - Get the **Genki PDFs from a library** as a backup reference when an explanation doesn't click — having both costs nothing and covers both teaching orders.

**Choose Genki instead if:** you want a single book that holds your hand chapter by
chapter and you don't mind buying it / don't trust yourself to self-direct. It's the
safer, more structured choice — and it's literally what first-year college students use.

## Bottom line

There's no wrong pick — thousands have reached fluency on each. For *your* setup,
**Tae Kim + an SRS for practice** is the free, efficient default. If you ever stall or
crave more structure, switch to or add Genki. Decide, start this week, don't agonize.
