# Paid Options Worth It

You want free until N4 unless something is *undeniably* superior. This page is the
honest flag-list: what's worth paying for, what it saves you, and the iOS Anki workaround.

> [!important] None of these are required
> You can reach N1 entirely free (see [[Free Resource Stack]]). These are accelerators
> and conveniences. Ranked by "actually worth it."

## Tier 1 — genuinely worth it

### AnkiMobile (iOS) — $24.99 one-time
- **What:** the official Anki app for iPhone/iPad. One-time, no subscription.
- **Why it might be worth it:** Anki is your daily-for-years backbone. On iOS the official app is the smoothest experience, syncs everywhere, fully offline.
- **The free workarounds (you asked):**
  1. **AnkiWeb in Safari** — free, syncs your decks, but clunky, no offline mode, slower. Fine to start.
  2. **Free iOS alternatives that import Anki decks** — apps like **MintDeck**, **Space**, **FlashRecall** import `.apkg` decks (cards, media, FSRS scheduling) for free with modern UIs. Quality varies; the ecosystem is newer than Anki.
  3. **Use Anki free on desktop/laptop, phone only for overflow** — if you study mostly at a desk, you may not need the iOS app at all.
- **Verdict:** Try the free route first. If you find yourself doing SRS on your phone daily and the web version annoys you, the $25 is the best money in this whole plan (it's once, forever).

### italki tutors — ~$5–15/lesson (only if you add speaking)
- **What:** 1-on-1 tutors/conversation partners by the lesson.
- **Why:** speaking is your #5 priority and self-study can't really build it. *If* you ever want output, nothing beats cheap real conversation. Skippable entirely if you stay input-only.
- **Free alternative:** **HelloTalk / Tandem** language exchange (you teach English, they help with Japanese). Free, less structured.

## Tier 2 — nice accelerators (optional)

### WaniKani — ~$9/mo or $299 lifetime (free to level 3)
- **What:** dedicated kanji + vocab SRS, mnemonic-driven (Tofugu team).
- **Why some swear by it:** removes all deck-management friction; just shows up and drills. Great if Anki's DIY nature annoys you.
- **Why you might skip:** learning kanji *inside vocab* (free, in Anki) covers the same ground. Redundant if you mine. Try free to level 3 and decide.

### Bunpro — ~$5/mo (free trial)
- **What:** SRS'd grammar, N5→N1, with example sentences and a clear path.
- **Why:** turns grammar into a trackable, reviewable system. Pairs perfectly with Tae Kim/Genki.
- **Free alternative:** put grammar points into Anki yourself (more work, same result).

### Satori Reader — ~$9/mo (free sample content)
- **What:** graded reading with integrated dictionary, audio, and grammar notes.
- **Why:** best-in-class bridge for the awkward N4→N3 reading gap. Genuinely excellent.

## Tier 3 — situational

- **Migaku (~$10/mo)** — Netflix/YouTube/browser sentence-mining suite. Slick, but Yomitan does the core for free.
- **Genki I & II textbooks (~$50 each new)** — the college standard; worth it if you want a structured book in hand. Used/library copies exist.
- **Shin Kanzen Master / Quartet / Tobira (~$25–40 each)** — the serious N3–N1 prep books. Worth buying when you actually reach those levels.

## What's NOT worth paying for (for your goals)

- ❌ **Duolingo Super** — gamified, shallow, won't get you near JLPT levels. Free version is a fine snack at most.
- ❌ **Rosetta Stone** — expensive, weak for Japanese specifically.
- ❌ **Most "learn Japanese fast" apps** — marketing over method.

## The recommendation

**Spend $0 through Stage 2 (N4).** If by then you're committed and studying daily on
your phone, the only near-automatic buy is **AnkiMobile ($25 once)**. Everything else —
WaniKani, Bunpro, italki, the N2/N1 prep books — buy *only when you hit the stage that
needs it*, and only if the free alternative is actively slowing you down.
