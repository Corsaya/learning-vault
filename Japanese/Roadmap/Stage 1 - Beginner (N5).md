# Stage 1 — Beginner (N5)

**Goal:** Core grammar foundation, first ~600–700 words, first ~100 kanji, and your
first real reading + listening. This is where the language starts to feel like a system.

**Hours:** to ~400 cumulative · **ETA:** ~1.1 yr @7hr · ~9 mo @10hr

## What you'll be able to do (can-do)

- Read and understand simple sentences in hiragana, katakana, and basic kanji
- Handle present/past, positive/negative, basic particles (は が を に で へ と も)
- Understand slow, simple conversation about everyday topics
- Read graded readers at level 0–1 and the easiest NHK Easy stories with lookups

## Targets

| Thing | Target |
|---|---|
| Grammar | All N5 grammar (Genki I ch. 1–12 OR Tae Kim "Basic Grammar") |
| Vocab | ~600–700 words |
| Kanji (recognition) | ~80–100 |
| Reading | Tadoku graded readers L0→L1 |
| Listening | Comprehensible-input beginner channels |

## The spine (pick ONE grammar path)

- **Path A — Textbook (recommended for structure):** **Genki I** (the college standard — this is literally what most US universities use for first-year Japanese). Work through chapters 1–12.
- **Path B — Free online:** **[Tae Kim's Guide](https://guidetojapanese.org/learn/)** (completely free, beloved, slightly less hand-holdy than Genki). Pair with Bunpro for SRS'd grammar.

Either way, **add Bunpro** (or its free analog) to drill grammar with SRS, and run a
**vocab SRS deck** (Anki "Kaishi 1.5k" — the modern recommended starter deck, free).

## Weekly emphasis (from [[Weekly Routine]])

~35% SRS · 30% grammar · 30% input · 5% output. Textbook-heavy stage — that's correct here.

## Kanji decision (do this now)

You ranked handwriting #3. Recommended approach:
- **Default: recognition-first.** Learn kanji *inside vocab cards* (you see 食べる, you learn to read it). This is the fast path and all the JLPT needs.
- **Optional skill track:** if handwriting matters to you, add **~10 min/week** writing the kanji you've already learned to read. Don't let it slow vocab acquisition. (Some people use WaniKani for kanji recognition — free to level 3; see [[Paid Options Worth It]].)

## Listening starts NOW (don't wait)

Even at near-zero, start ~30 min/week of beginner comprehensible input:
- **[Comprehensible Japanese](https://www.youtube.com/@cijapanese)** (YouTube, graded by level, free)
- **Nihongo con Teppei (Beginner)** podcast (free)
Understanding 10% is fine. You're training your ear's runway.

## Milestone checklist

- [ ] Finished N5 grammar (Genki I or Tae Kim basics)
- [ ] ~600+ vocab in SRS, reviewing daily
- [ ] ~80+ kanji recognized in context
- [ ] Read 5+ Tadoku L0 graded readers
- [ ] Watched/listened to 10+ beginner CI videos/episodes
- [ ] Can read one NHK Easy article with a dictionary
- [ ] (Optional) Can handwrite the ~50 most common kanji you've learned

## Self-assessment (no exam needed)

Try a **free N5 mock test** ([JLPT official samples](https://www.jlpt.jp/e/samples/sample12.html) or Coto/jlptsensei mocks). Comfortably passing the reading/listening sections = you're ready.

## Done when…

N5 grammar complete, ~700 words, reading easy graded material with a dictionary, and
a free N5 mock feels comfortable. → [[Roadmap/Stage 2 - Upper Beginner (N4)|Stage 2]].
