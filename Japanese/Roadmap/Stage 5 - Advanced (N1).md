# Stage 5 — Advanced (N1)

**Goal:** Mastery. Understand Japanese across formal, literary, academic, and abstract
registers — newspapers, novels, business, dense nonfiction. This stage is **almost
entirely volume**: huge amounts of varied reading and listening, plus targeted drilling
of N1's notoriously obscure grammar and vocabulary.

**Hours:** to ~3,600+ cumulative · **ETA:** ~9.9 yr @7hr · **~6.9 yr @10hr** (your target)

> [!note] This is the 10-hr/week stage
> The N1→ gap is what makes the difference between a ~7-year and ~10-year finish. If
> N1-in-7-years is the real goal, this is where sustained ~10 hrs/week matters most.
> See [[Hours & Timeline Reality Check]].

## What you'll be able to do (can-do)

- Read newspapers, novels, academic papers, and business documents with ease
- Understand essentially any spoken Japanese (news, lectures, fast natural speech)
- Grasp nuance, idiom, formal/literary register, and subtext
- Function in academic or professional Japanese contexts

## Targets

| Thing | Target |
|---|---|
| Grammar | All N1 grammar (rare/formal/written-only constructions) |
| Vocab | ~10,000+ total |
| Kanji (recognition) | ~2,000+ (effectively all jōyō) |
| Reading | Newspapers, novels, nonfiction, academic prose |
| Listening | Any native content, including news/lectures/dialects |

## The spine — volume + targeted N1 prep

- **Grammar/vocab drilling:** **Shin Kanzen Master N1** series (grammar, vocab, reading, listening) — the gold-standard N1 prep. Much of N1 grammar is rare written forms you'll rarely *see* in casual immersion, so explicit study is genuinely necessary here.
- **Immersion:** wide and varied — deliberately read *outside* your comfort genres (news, essays, classic literature, technical writing) to cover N1's register range.
- **Mining:** continue; the well of unknown words is deep but slowing.

## Weekly emphasis

~20% SRS · 5% grammar (concentrated N1 drilling) · 70% input · 5% output. **Mostly
consumption,** with focused N1-prep sprints before any mock/exam.

## The reading endgame (your #1 priority, fully realized)

This is where "formal reading" peaks. Build a habit of:
- A daily news article (Asahi/NHK/Yomiuri) read at speed
- A long-form novel or nonfiction book in progress at all times
- Periodic "hard" texts (editorials, academic abstracts) to stretch register

## Milestone checklist

- [ ] Finished N1 grammar (Shin Kanzen Master N1 or equivalent)
- [ ] ~10,000+ vocab
- [ ] ~2,000+ kanji recognized
- [ ] Read multiple full novels and a nonfiction book
- [ ] Read a daily newspaper comfortably
- [ ] Understand news broadcasts and lectures without aids
- [ ] Passed full N1 mock tests consistently (Shin Kanzen / official samples)
- [ ] (If you choose) **Sat the real JLPT N1** — the mastery credential

## Done when…

You pass N1 mocks consistently and Japanese media across registers is simply...
readable and listenable. That's mastery. If you sit and pass the real N1 — that's the
formal summit. 🎌

## After N1

Maintenance, not study: keep consuming what you love. Optionally add output mastery
(speaking/writing) if you skipped it — at N1 comprehension, output comes fast. Consider
specialized vocab (business, a field you care about) or translation as a next mountain.
