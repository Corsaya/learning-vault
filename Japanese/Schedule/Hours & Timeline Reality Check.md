# Hours & Timeline Reality Check

The honest math. No hype. This is what the research actually says about how long
Japanese takes for an English speaker with **no prior kanji background**.

## Where the numbers come from

- **FSI** (US Foreign Service Institute) ranks Japanese a **Category IV / "super-hard" language** — its hardest tier — at roughly **2,200 class hours** for general professional proficiency, the most of any language they teach.
- Published JLPT study-hour estimates (Coto Academy, Migaku, JLPT Bootcamp, Nihongo-Career) cluster as follows for learners *without* a kanji background:

| Level | Cumulative hours (range) | Midpoint used here |
|---|---|---|
| Kana + setup | 20–30 | **25** |
| N5 | 350–460 | **400** |
| N4 | 575–1,000 | **800** |
| N3 | 950–1,700 | **1,400** |
| N2 | 1,600–2,800 | **2,200** |
| N1 | 3,000–4,800 | **3,600** |

These are *cumulative from zero*, not per-level. They assume reasonably efficient
study. People with Chinese/Korean backgrounds go much faster; you and I should
plan with these honest "no shortcut" numbers and be pleasantly surprised.

## Your pace → your timeline

- **7 hrs/week = 364 hrs/year** (sustainable default)
- **10 hrs/week = 520 hrs/year** (committed track)

| Milestone | Cumul. hrs | @ 7 hr/wk | @ 10 hr/wk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Kana done | 25 | ~5 weeks | ~3.5 weeks |
| **N5** | 400 | ~1.1 years | ~9 months |
| **N4** | 800 | ~2.2 years | ~1.5 years |
| **N3** | 1,400 | ~3.8 years | ~2.7 years |
| **N2** | 2,200 | ~6.0 years | ~4.2 years |
| **N1** | 3,600 | ~9.9 years | **~6.9 years** |

### The bottom line

- At **10 hrs/week sustained, N1 is realistic in ~7 years** — exactly your target.
- At **7 hrs/week, N1 is ~10 years**, but **N2 in ~6 years** is very strong (N2 is the level most employers and universities actually ask for).
- For comparison, your original **3 hrs/week** would have reached N1 in ~23 years and landed at ~N3 by year 8. The jump to 7–10 is what makes the goal real.

## The realistic "ramp" path (recommended)

Don't force 10 hrs/week on day one. Most people who sustain high hours got there
because immersion became *fun*, not because they white-knuckled it.

1. **Stage 0–early N5:** 7 hrs/week. Build the habit. Make the streak unbreakable.
2. **Mid N5 onward:** as anime/manga immersion starts counting as "study," hours rise naturally. Push toward 10.
3. **N3+:** if you're still in, you'll likely *want* 10+ because you can finally enjoy native content. This is where the timeline accelerates on its own.

A blended path (7 → ramping to 10 around N3) realistically puts **N1 around years 7–8** — your goal, achieved by getting hooked, not by grinding.

## Two things that bend the curve in your favor

- **Immersion double-counts as life.** Watching a subbed anime *actively* (paying attention to words, occasionally looking one up) is both entertainment and study hours. A meaningful slice of your weekly total is stuff you'd half-do anyway.
- **The JLPT tests only reading + listening.** No speaking or handwriting on the exam. Since those are your lower priorities anyway, you're not paying the (large) time tax of handwriting mastery unless you choose to.

## Two things that work against you

- **Kanji volume.** ~2,000+ kanji for N1. This is the single biggest time sink and why "no kanji background" roughly doubles the hours vs. a Chinese speaker. SRS is non-negotiable because of this.
- **The intermediate plateau (N3→N2).** Progress *feels* like it stops because each new word appears less often. This is where most quitting happens. Expect it; it's not you failing.
