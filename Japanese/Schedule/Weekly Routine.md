# Weekly Routine

How to actually spend the hours. The split shifts as you advance (more input, less
textbook), but the **daily SRS habit is constant and non-negotiable**.

> [!important] The one rule
> **SRS reviews happen every single day**, even on a 5-minute day. Miss a day and
> reviews pile up and the streak psychology breaks. Everything else can flex; this can't.

---

## Default: 7 hours/week (≈ 60 min/day, 1 lighter day)

A daily structure beats weekend cramming. Target ~1 hour/day, 6 days, 1 rest day —
but split however fits school/work/crew.

| Block | Time/week | What | When |
|---|---|---|---|
| **SRS reviews** | ~140 min (20/day) | Vocab + kanji recognition flashcards | Every day, ideally morning |
| **Grammar** | ~70 min | New grammar points in small bites (Tae Kim → Genki → Bunpro) | 2–3 sessions |
| **Listening / immersion** | ~90 min | Active watching/listening, slightly above level | 3–4 sessions |
| **Reading** | ~70 min | Graded readers → NHK Easy → native (your #1 priority) | 2–3 sessions |
| **Output (optional)** | ~30 min | Handwriting drills OR light speaking shadowing | 1–2 sessions |
| **Flex / review** | ~20 min | Catch-up, weak spots, re-watch | as needed |

**= ~420 min (7 hrs).** Reading + listening get the most non-SRS time, matching your priorities.

### A sample week (7 hr)

- **Mon** — SRS (20) + new grammar (25) + reading (15) → 60
- **Tue** — SRS (20) + immersion/listening (30) + reading (10) → 60
- **Wed** — SRS (20) + grammar (20) + handwriting (20) → 60
- **Thu** — SRS (20) + immersion (30) + reading (10) → 60
- **Fri** — SRS (20) + reading (25) + grammar review (15) → 60
- **Sat** — SRS (20) + immersion (30) + flex (10) → 60
- **Sun** — SRS only (20) — rest day → 20

## Committed: 10 hours/week (≈ 85 min/day)

Same skeleton, more input. The extra ~3 hrs goes almost entirely to **immersion and reading**, because at every level past N5 that's what compounds fastest.

| Block | Time/week | Change vs 7 hr |
|---|---|---|
| SRS reviews | ~175 min (25/day) | +35 (you'll have more cards) |
| Grammar | ~80 min | +10 |
| Listening / immersion | ~150 min | +60 |
| Reading | ~120 min | +50 |
| Output (optional) | ~40 min | +10 |
| Flex / review | ~35 min | +15 |

**= ~600 min (10 hrs).** This is the pace that puts N1 in ~7 years.

---

## How the split shifts by stage

| Stage | SRS | Grammar | Input (read+listen) | Output |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **0 — Kana** | kana drills | none | none | tracing kana |
| **1 — N5** | 35% | 30% | 30% | 5% |
| **2 — N4** | 35% | 20% | 40% | 5% |
| **3 — N3** | 30% | 15% | 50% | 5% |
| **4 — N2** | 25% | 10% | 60% | 5% |
| **5 — N1** | 20% | 5% | 70% | 5% |

The arc: **textbook-heavy → immersion-heavy.** By N2 you're mostly *living in* Japanese
content and SRS-ing what you mine from it, with grammar as occasional reference.

## Using NotebookLM (your audio/video engine)

You said you'll use NotebookLM for audio/video output. Concrete ways:

- **Audio Overviews of grammar notes** → paste a grammar chapter's notes, generate a podcast-style audio, listen on commutes/erg for passive reinforcement.
- **Drill summaries** → feed it your weak-point list, ask for a spoken Q&A review.
- **Source-grounded Q&A** → load a textbook chapter PDF and quiz yourself by asking it questions.
- ⚠️ Don't use it to *generate Japanese sentences to learn from* early on — LLM-generated Japanese can be subtly unnatural. Learn from native/graded material; use NotebookLM to review *explanations*, not as a source of correct Japanese.

## Protecting the habit

- **Stack it on an existing routine** (e.g., SRS right after morning coffee, immersion during cooldown after crew).
- **A bad day = SRS only.** 5 minutes counts. Never zero.
- **Log it** in [[Log]] — even one line. Visible streaks are motivating.
- **Weekly review (Sun, 5 min):** glance at [[Master Checklist]], note what's next.
