## Was the draft actually bad?

**Yes. The first four rounds were bad enough to determine the current roster's position.** The later draft was considerably better.

Team Gassy's opening picks were:

1. Josh Allen — 1.01
2. Travis Hunter — 2.08
3. Cameron Dicker — 3.01
4. Los Angeles Rams DEF — 4.08

In a one-QB, eight-team league, Allen at 1.01 was a major opportunity-cost loss. Lamar Jackson lasted until Round 4, Joe Burrow until Round 5, and Jalen Hurts and Drake Maye until Round 8. Several viable quarterbacks were still unrostered after the draft.

Hunter at 2.08 produced a current Week 1 projection below three points because Sleeper lists him primarily at DB and his reported offensive role is limited. A kicker in Round 3 and defense in Round 4 sacrificed two more premium RB/WR selections at positions where comparable weekly options remained on waivers.

That four-round opening explains most of the gap between Team Gassy and LeGOAT. LeGOAT converted its first four selections into Jahmyr Gibbs, Christian McCaffrey, Amon-Ra St. Brown, and Nico Collins.

The rest of Team Gassy's draft was not uniformly bad:

- Ladd McConkey in Round 5 was useful.
- Jameson Williams in Round 12 was one of the roster's best values.
- Sam LaPorta in Round 13 was strong value.
- TreVeyon Henderson retains upside if healthy.
- David Montgomery is usable, although not enough to carry the RB room alone.

Overall draft grade: **D**. That grade is driven by the first four rounds, not every selection.

## Is there hope?

**Yes, but not from waiting for this exact roster to outperform its projections.** The fixed-roster path produces only a 16.75% playoff rate. The recovery path is active waiver management in a shallow league.

As of the projection snapshot, unrostered players included Rhamondre Stevenson, Jayden Reed, Jordan Mason, Rachaad White, DJ Moore, Jordan Addison, Courtland Sutton, and Tucker Kraft. The best immediate structural moves are to use Henderson's IR eligibility when Sleeper permits it, turn Hunter's low-output bench slot into RB/WR production, and avoid carrying Kincaid indefinitely behind LaPorta unless a trade is realistic.

Merely replacing Hunter with Rhamondre Stevenson raises the projected Week 2–14 optimal lineup by roughly **0.7 points per week**. Adding both Stevenson and Jayden Reed in place of Hunter and Kincaid raises it by approximately **1.0 point per week**. That helps, but it does not erase the full gap; at least one current RB must beat projection or a stronger waiver/trade opportunity must emerge.

The practical target is not catching LeGOAT immediately. It is closing the roughly **9–11 weekly-point gap** between Team Gassy and the fourth-through-seventh cluster. One breakout RB plus competent weekly waiver decisions can do that. Passive roster management probably cannot.

## Model and data

The model executed **200,000 season paths** using:

- Sleeper's live Week 1 rosters after the Sadiq-to-Metcalf transaction
- the actual Lil' Oats Week 1–14 matchup schedule
- Sleeper/Rotowire half-PPR player projections for Weeks 1–16
- Week 1 points already posted when the snapshot was captured
- legal QB/RB/WR/TE/FLEX/K/DEF lineup selection
- persistent player-level projection uncertainty
- position-specific weekly scoring variance
- correlated weekly outcomes for players on the same NFL team
- modeled multiweek injuries and known bye weeks
- record-first playoff seeding with points-for as the simulated tiebreaker
- four playoff teams, with 1-vs.-4 and 2-vs.-3 semifinals in Week 15 and a Week 16 final

All rosters remain fixed after the snapshot. The simulation does not model waivers, trades, commissioner intervention, intentional tanking, lineup neglect, or managers learning from early-season roles.

Monte Carlo sampling error is below approximately **0.2 percentage points** for the headline Team Gassy playoff estimate. Model and projection uncertainty is much larger than sampling error, so 16.75% should be read as approximately 17% under these assumptions—not as actuarial truth.
