# OEM Controller First Week and Main Trial

## Call for this trial

Start with **Falco**. He is the best fit for the stated objective: learn portable Melee fundamentals while committing to one character. Existing recordings already show usable Falco and Jigglypuff reps. Falco's core loop—movement, spacing, short-hop laser, aerial timing, defense, and punish restraint—rewards the exact fundamentals being built.

This is not a claim that Falco is objectively "the best" character. Jigglypuff is viable and simpler mechanically, but her air-first game transfers less directly to grounded movement. Pikachu is a smaller-resource, technical commitment. Yoshi is the worst first-main fit here: parry, double-jump armor, and no conventional shield make his learning curve character-specific.

## Arrival check — 15 minutes

Before drilling, complete the controller's [[Equipment/Nintendo GameCube Controller - Platinum Silver DOL-003#Arrival inspection|arrival inspection]]: neutral return, both dash-dance directions, all buttons, trigger analog travel/digital click, and drift/snapback. Preserve the return window until the basic test is clean.

Do not remap inputs or chase notches on day one. Use the OEM layout long enough to distinguish unfamiliarity from a controller fault.

## Daily 45-minute block, days 1–7

Use UnclePunch with input display visible for the first 20 minutes. Stop a drill when form collapses; fast bad repetitions teach the bad input.

| Minutes | Drill | Pass condition |
|---:|---|---|
| 0–5 | Free movement on Battlefield: walk, dash, dash dance, platforms, turn around | No accidental crouches, rolls, or full hops for one minute |
| 5–10 | 10 short hops, then 10 short-hop fast-falls, each direction | 8/10 short hops in each set |
| 10–15 | 20 L-cancelled neutral-airs and down-airs | 16/20 clean landings; reset the count after obvious missed timing |
| 15–20 | 20 wavedashes: 10 each direction | 8/10 move in the intended direction without an air dodge upward |
| 20–30 | Falco short-hop laser: stationary, forward, then retreating | 8/10 low lasers in each version; prioritize low and controlled over rapid |
| 30–40 | One defensive drill: tech in place/left/right, then DI escape/recovery routes | Correct option 8/10; no attack button during the escape rep |
| 40–45 | One ranked/unranked game or two-minute movement flow | Write one repeated input error and one decision error |

### Daily emphasis

1. **Day 1:** Controller inspection; short hop and dash-dance feel only. Skip lasers if the controller setup is not confirmed.
2. **Day 2:** Add fast-fall and L-cancel; do not grind more than 20 clean attempts per aerial.
3. **Day 3:** Add wavedash and waveland. It is a movement option, not a required approach.
4. **Day 4:** Falco short-hop laser. The goal is one laser that gives information before entering, not a laser waterfall.
5. **Day 5:** Teching, DI, and recovery. In a match, deliberately reset after getting hit rather than instantly swinging back.
6. **Day 6:** Play 3–5 games as Falco. Match rule: every committed approach begins after a laser, dash-dance bait, or clear whiff.
7. **Day 7:** Record three full games and review only: (a) unforced execution misses, (b) direct approaches, and (c) times an extension stopped being guaranteed.

## Videos — watch in this order

1. [How to L-Cancel — Learning Melee From Scratch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fzNwTb6_pFI) — short hop, fast fall, L-cancel, and a practice method in one video.
2. [How To Wavedash — Learning Melee From Scratch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6UuPoSUrp18) — learn the input and its relation to dash dance; do not promote ledgedash to the daily block yet.
3. [How To Practice Falco — SSBM Tutorials](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=15myVh03yos) — use this after the first three days, when the controller no longer feels foreign.
4. [The Melee Library](https://www.meleelibrary.com/) — a curated index for DI, teching, neutral, and later character-specific questions. Use it to find one answer at a time, not to build a 30-video backlog.

## Main decision on day 7

Play the same opponent or UnclePunch scenario for 20 minutes each with Falco, Jigglypuff, Pikachu, and Yoshi. Score each 1–5 on:

- desire to play another set after losing;
- ability to name why a stock was lost;
- number of controller-input errors; and
- whether the character's basic gameplan is enjoyable without advanced techniques.

Choose the highest total and lock that main for **30 days**. Default tiebreaker: **Falco**. A 30-day commitment is long enough for adaptation and short enough that it is not a life sentence if the character is genuinely wrong.

## Guardrails

- No ledgedash grind, multi-shine, shield drop, or character-specific advanced tech during this week.
- Do not measure progress by wins. Measure clean input rate and whether approaches begin with information instead of blind commitment.
- The existing review's most relevant rule survives the controller switch: `hit -> reposition -> punish the escape`.
